## 2. Your lifts

3 sets of each. Stop 1 or 2 reps before you would fail. When all 3 sets reach the top number, go up one weight step next time.

| Push (Mon, Thu) | Reps | Pull (Tue, Fri) | Reps | Legs (Wed, Sat) | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seated chest press | 8–12 | Lat pulldown | 8–12 | Leg press | 10–15 |
| Seated pec fly | 10–15 | Seated row | 8–12 | Leg extension | 10–15 |
| Seated shoulder press | 8–12 | Rear delt fly | 12–15 | Seated leg curl | 10–15 |
| Seated triceps extension | 10–15 | Biceps curl | 10–15 | Calf raise on the leg press | 12–15 |

- Rest about 2 minutes between sets on the presses, pulldown, row and leg press. About 1 minute on the others.
- Calf raises: slow, with a 2-second pause at the bottom. They get your Achilles ready for the incline.
- Write down the weight and reps every session. Beating last week by one rep is progress.
- Easy week, any time your joints ache or every lift feels heavy: same weights, 2 sets, for one week.

## 5. Walking

Keep the 10,000 steps a day. That is about 100 minutes at speed 3.0, and it is already working. On your four upper-body days, part of that walk is now on an incline. You do not walk any longer than you do today.

- **Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri:** walk flat for 5 minutes, do the incline block from the Section 6 table, then finish your steps flat. Speed 3.0, hands off the rails. The block counts toward the 10,000.
- **Wed, Sat, Sun:** all flat. Your legs and calves get worked on leg days.
- The incline is the number on the treadmill. It starts at 5 and goes up by 1 at most each week, to 12. You never need 15.
- The table is the most you do, not a must. Move to the next line only if last week was pain-free and you could talk in full sentences the whole time (heart rate under 150 if you can see it). If not, repeat your line. Repeating is normal. Many people your size stay between 7 and 10 for months. You do not need 12 to hit your weight goal.
- Cannot talk, need the rails, or heart rate over 160: drop the incline by 2 for the rest of that week.
- Why add it: incline 8 burns about twice as much per minute as flat, and 12 about 2.5 times as much. Thirty minutes at incline 8 adds roughly 150 to 200 calories on those days.
- Short on time: each minute of your incline block at 8 or higher counts as 2 flat minutes, so you can skip one flat minute for each. Never make the block longer to save time.

## 7. When to change something

| If this happens | Do this |
|---|---|
| You lose more than 1% of your weight a week (2.7 lb now, 2.3 lb at 230), 2 weeks in a row | Add 200 calories a day. Keep them while you still lose 1.5 lb a week or more. Still too fast 2 weeks later: add 200 more |
| You lose less than 1 lb a week, 2 weeks in a row | Weigh all your food for a week. Still slow: drop 100 calories. Never under 2,000 |
| Bad sleep, no energy, dizzy when you stand up, starving most days or lifts dropping for 2 weeks in a row, or two of those in the same week | Add 200 calories a day for 2 weeks. Still wrecked after that: take a break week |
| Calf, Achilles, shin, foot, knee or lower-back pain 4 out of 10 or worse on a walk, or stiff over 15 minutes next morning | All flat for 7 days. Pain gone: restart the incline 2 lower than where you were. If it hurts on flat walks too, or is still there after the 7 days: halve your steps and get it checked |
| Chest pain, fainting, or a pop or sharp pain in a tendon | Stop. Get it checked |

**Break weeks are not on the schedule.** A break week is 7 days at your current calories plus 600, with flat walking only. It is a break from the diet, not from the gym. Take one when the table above says so, or over Christmas if you want one. Each one moves every later row back a week and costs about 2 lb by May.

## 8. The finish

You are done when your weekly average is 200 lb. Do not go straight back to eating freely. Raise calories 150 a week for 4 weeks: 2,250, then 2,400, then 2,550, then 2,700, with protein at 200 g. Then hold there.
