### The Ten Non-Negotiables

1. Hit calories within 50 kcal and protein within 10 g every day. Weigh your food.
2. Never eat below 2,000 kcal. Never add cardio beyond the schedule.
3. Lift six days a week with the logbook open. Lifting is never the session that gets cut for time. Only illness or a Section 1.8 stop sign cancels it (Section 4.6).
4. Hands off the treadmill rails. If you need them, drop a ladder level.
5. No incline on a leg day, never more than two incline days in a row, never more than 40 minutes.
6. Weigh in every morning. Decide only on Sunday, only from the 7-day average.
7. Take every scheduled diet-break week at the prescribed maintenance calories.
8. Pain at 4 out of 10 or more, or morning stiffness beyond 15 minutes, starts the Tendon Protocol that day.
9. One gallon of water, 3,000–4,000 mg of sodium (2,300 mg if your blood pressure reads 130/80 or higher, Section 1.8), 8–9 hours in bed.
10. When the flags say add 200 kcal or take a break, do it. Following that rule is part of the discipline.

### 1.3 Safe Rate of Loss and Managing Metabolic Adaptation

> **Rule:** Lose 1.75–2.25 lb/week on the 7-day average, and never more than 1.0% of body weight per week for 2 weeks running.

1.0% is about 2.7 lb at 270, 2.5 at 250 and 2.3 at 230. Inside 1.75–2.25 lb/week is ideal; 1.5–1.75 is acceptable and needs no change. The responses to losing too fast (flag F6) and too slowly (the plateau rule) are in Section 1.5.

Losing faster does not mean losing more fat. Past the guardrail the extra pounds cost you:

| Cost of losing faster | What happens |
|---|---|
| Muscle | A larger share of each pound lost is lean tissue |
| Strength | Indicator lifts stall, then slide (flag F2) |
| Tendons | Less recovery between incline sessions |
| Hunger and sleep | Both get worse (flags F4 and F1), and adherence follows |
| Metabolism | A larger adaptive slowdown |
| Gallbladder | Very rapid loss raises gallstone risk |

**What metabolic adaptation is.** As you lose weight you burn less. Most of that is physics: a smaller body costs less to run and to move, roughly 100–130 kcal/day per 15 lb. Adaptation is the extra drop on top: you move less without noticing, hunger signals rise and resting burn dips slightly. Early in a cut it is modest, on the order of 100 kcal/day as an estimate, and the model budgets about 100. It tends to grow with the size of the loss: after 20% or more of body weight it can reach 200–300 kcal/day (estimate), most of it from moving less, so expect the later tiers to run toward the slow end of their ranges. It grows faster with crash deficits, low protein, no lifting and short sleep. The plan removes each of those.

| Tool | Setting | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Milestone-sized steps | −100 kcal per 15 lb milestone; never below 2,000 kcal | Tracks the real TDEE drop, so no crash cut is ever needed |
| Protein + lifting | 210 g/day; 6 sessions/week | Holds lean mass, which holds resting burn and strength |
| Step floor | 8,000 steps/day | Blocks the unnoticed drop in daily movement, usually the largest piece |
| Maintenance weeks | Weeks 6, 13 and 20; Weeks 26 and 33 in Phase 2 | Refills glycogen, restores training quality, lowers hunger and fatigue |
| Sleep | 8–9 hours in bed | Short sleep raises hunger and shifts loss away from fat |
| Capped cardio | 4 sessions/week, 40 min hard cap | More cardio buys more hunger and less movement the rest of the day |

**What a diet-break week is.** Seven days at estimated maintenance: your current tier + 600 kcal, with protein at 200 g, fat at 80 g and the rest from carbs. That is 2,900 kcal in Week 6, 2,800 in Week 13 and 2,700 in Week 20 (Section 2.3); the Phase 2 breaks are in Section 7.3. The estimate is conservative, so it sits at or just under true maintenance.

| A diet-break week is | It is not |
|---|---|
| Maintenance calories at the Section 2.3 numbers | Untracked eating or a free week |
| Weighed, logged and high-protein (200 g) | A week off the food scale or the morning weigh-in |

**What the scale does.** Expect it to rise 1–3 lb during the week from glycogen, water and food volume. It is not fat, and it comes off within 5–10 days of resuming the deficit. The on-track range in Section 2.3 already carries 2 lb of headroom for break weeks, and the too-fast check ignores the first week back.

The evidence that maintenance weeks reverse adaptation itself is mixed. The dependable returns are better training, lower hunger and fatigue, and an unloaded Achilles. Those are reason enough to keep all three.

### 1.4 Incline Walking Without Wrecking Your Achilles, Calves or Lower Back

> **Rule:** An incline walk in this plan is 15% grade at 3.0 mph with your hands off the rails, for the minutes in Section 2.4 and never more than 40 in one session. If you cannot do it hands-free and under the heart-rate ceiling, work at a lower level of the Incline Ladder and keep the minutes.

Incline walking is your main cardio tool and the most likely source of an overuse injury in this plan. Heart and lungs adapt in weeks; tendons take months. The schedule sets the dose, not how fresh you feel.

#### Why 15% at 3.0 mph is a serious load at 270 lb

| Factor | At 15% and 3.0 mph | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | About 9.5 METs (about 33 ml/kg/min of oxygen) | Vigorous work, close to jogging intensity |
| Energy cost | About 15.5 kcal/min at 270 lb | Flat walking at 3.0 mph costs about 4–5 kcal/min |
| Ankle | More dorsiflexion (shin closing toward the toes) on every step | Calf and Achilles work at a longer length under load |
| Lower back | Sustained forward lean | The erectors (the muscles along your spine) hold your trunk all session and fatigue |
| Rails | Holding on cuts the cost by roughly a quarter or more | It also wrecks your posture |

The kcal figures are planning estimates, set deliberately below the standard walking equation. The treadmill console reads high; ignore it.

**Ankle, calf and Achilles.** A 15% grade tilts the belt about 8.5°. With your heel down, the ankle bends roughly that much further than on flat ground, so the calf and Achilles are stretched longer while they carry your weight and push it uphill. A 30-minute session is roughly 3,000 of those steps (estimate). Load per step scales with body weight: about 11% less at 240 lb and about 17% less at 225 lb than at 270 lb. That is why the minutes rise as your weight falls.

**Lower back.** You lean forward to stay balanced on the slope, and your lower-back muscles hold that lean all session. Lean from the ankles and they cope. Fold at the hips and they fatigue fast.

**Rails.** Hang back on the rails and your body ends up square to the belt: the hill is gone and the console still counts it. Your arms carry part of your weight, and your spine sits arched and locked while it tires.

#### Hands-free form

1. Hands off the rails; arms swing naturally. If you need the rails, the level is too high. Clip the emergency-stop key to your shirt before the belt starts.
2. Lean forward slightly from the ankles, not by folding at the hips. Ribs stacked over pelvis, light brace.
3. Whole foot lands; your heel touches down every step. No walking on your toes.
4. Shorter, quicker steps than on flat ground. Push the belt back with your glutes.
5. Eyes forward, chest tall, stay in the middle of the belt.
6. Shoes: cushioned running or walking shoes with a normal 8–12 mm heel-to-toe drop. Not flat lifting shoes.

Cue 3 matters most for the Achilles: toe-walking keeps the calf loaded with no release between steps. Cue 6 follows the same logic: a raised heel takes stretch off the tendon, so change shoes if you lift in flat ones. Touching a rail for a second to change a setting or take a pulse reading is fine. Carrying weight on it is not.

#### The Incline Ladder

> **Rule:** The scheduled minutes stay the same at every level. Hands-free at a lower level always beats rail-holding at Level 5.

| Level | Grade | Speed | Approx. intensity | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (target) | 15% | 3.0 mph | 9.5 METs | The level you build toward |
| 4 | 15% | 2.7 mph | 8.7 METs | Breathing or heart rate is the limit |
| 3 | 12% | 3.0 mph | 8.3 METs | Calf, Achilles or lower back is the limit |
| 2 | 10% | 3.0 mph | 7.4 METs | Still limited at Level 3; first step back after the Tendon Protocol |
| 1 | 8% | 3.0 mph | 6.6 METs | Still limited at Level 2 |
| 0 | 6% | 3.0 mph | 5.8 METs | Still limited at Level 1 |

**Heart-rate guardrail.** Working zone 125–155 bpm. Hard ceiling 160 bpm. Talk test: you can speak a full sentence. Your estimated maximum is about 195 bpm (208 − 0.7 × age). Read it from a watch or chest strap if you have one. The treadmill grip sensors are a rough check only: grip for 10 seconds, read, let go. With no reliable number, the talk test decides.

**Step down a level** when any one of these is true:

1. Heart rate stays above 160 bpm for more than 2 minutes.
2. You cannot speak a full sentence.
3. You need the rails to stay on.

**Which way to step down:**

- Breathing or heart rate is the limit: drop to Level 4 first.
- Calf, Achilles or lower back is the limit: skip Level 4 and drop the grade (Level 3, 2, 1 or 0). A slower belt does not change the ankle angle.

**Below Level 0.** Still over a limit at Level 0: drop to 4% at 3.0 mph, keep the minutes and write the grade in the Ladder level column (Section 6.2). Raise it at your doctor's visit (Section 1.8).

After a step-down, start the next session at the lower level.

**Step up one level** after 2 consecutive sessions in which you completed every prescribed minute hands-free, with heart rate under the ceiling and pain at 2/10 or less. Wait at least 7 days between step-ups to a level you have not worked at before: tendons adapt more slowly than heart and lungs.

**Optional, from Tier 4 onward only:** 3.2 mph at 15% if 3.0 mph feels like 6/10 effort or less with heart rate under 150 bpm.

**Starting point: find your level.** Level 5 is the level you build toward, not the starting line. Hands-free at 270 lb it takes about as much oxygen as most untrained people your size can deliver at full effort (estimate), so expect to start lower. If you have been walking 15% at 3.0 mph with your hands on the rails, you have not done Level 5 yet. On Monday of Week 1, after 1 min at 5%:

1. Walk 4 min at Level 1 (8%, 3.0 mph), hands-free. Read your heart rate and run the talk test.
2. Under 150 bpm and speaking in full sentences: go up one level and repeat.
3. Stop climbing at the first level that takes you past 150 bpm or breaks the talk test. Your working level is the one below it. Finish the session there; the test minutes count.
4. If Level 1 itself takes you past 150 bpm or breaks the talk test, work at Level 0. If Level 0 still puts you over the 160 bpm ceiling, slow it to 2.7 mph and raise it at your doctor's visit (Section 1.8).

Expect to land around Level 0–2 at this weight (estimate). That is the plan working: the heart-rate ceiling sets the level, and the level rises as you get fitter and lighter. Level 5 may take months to reach.

**Starting minutes.** If you currently do less incline than Week 1 prescribes, start at what you do now and add 5 min per session each week until you catch the table. If you do more, you still follow the table.

#### Every incline session, in order

| Step | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prehab | 2 × 30-second standing calf isometric holds per leg (mid-range, on the floor or a step), 10 slow ankle circles each way, 10 bodyweight glute bridges | 3 min |
| 2. Ramp-up | 1 min at 5%, 1 min at 10%, 1 min at 12% | 3 min, not counted |
| 3. Work | 15% at 3.0 mph, or your current ladder level, hands-free | Prescribed minutes |
| 4. Ramp-down | The ramp-up in reverse | 3 min, not counted |
| 5. Stretch | Gentle calf stretch, 2 × 30 seconds per side: knee straight, then knee bent | About 2 min |

- Never bounce the stretch and never stretch into Achilles pain.
- On a lower ladder level, stop the ramp at your working grade.
- On a day your back feels stiff, add before step 1: 6 bird-dogs per side, 6 dead bugs per side, 20-second side plank per side. Do the same before every leg day.

The isometric holds load the tendon without the stretch and recoil of walking. Long-term protection is built on leg days (Section 4): straight-knee calf work on Legs A, bent-knee (soleus) calf work on Legs B, both with a 3-second lowering phase and a 2-second pause in the stretch, plus 2 × 15–20 wall-leaning tibialis raises after each leg day. Slow, heavy calf work raises what the tendon can tolerate, and the incline spends it.

| Built into the schedule | What it does |
|---|---|
| Incline is never done on a leg day. Wed and Sat are flat recovery walks (0–3%, 3.0 mph; 2.5–3.0 on sore days) | Keeps the hill off calves, hamstrings and erectors already fatigued by Legs A and Legs B |

| Minutes rise 5 at a time | Small steps the tendon can absorb |
| Diet-break weeks (6, 13 and 20; 26 and 33 in Phase 2): no incline above 5% at all. Flat walk 30 min Mon–Fri | A planned tendon unload every 6–7 weeks |
| First week after each deload is 5 min shorter than the previous block's peak (Week 7 = 25 min, Week 14 = 30, Week 21 = 35; Weeks 27 and 34 = 35) | A graded return after a week off the hill |
| 40 minutes is the hard cap per session | See below |

Week-by-week minutes are in Section 2.4. How cardio sits around each workout is in Section 4.6.

**Why 60-minute sessions are not programmed.** At about 9.5 METs the extra 20 minutes costs more in Achilles load, recovery and hunger than it returns, and the deficit comes mostly from food. In numbers: 20 more minutes at 240 lb is about 275 kcal per session, about 1,100 kcal across four sessions, roughly 0.3 lb a week. The price is 50% more loaded steps in every session.

#### Pain scale

Score calf, Achilles, heel, foot, shin, knee and lower-back pain 0–10 during the walk, an hour after and the next morning. Log it on the Section 6.2 sheet. Score it with no painkillers on board: do not take ibuprofen or naproxen to get through a session, because they hide the signal this scale runs on.

| Level | What it looks like | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | 0–2/10 during, gone within an hour, nothing next morning | Train as written |
| Amber | 3/10 during that settles within 24 hours; OR any pain still there an hour after; OR next-morning stiffness or pain of 15 minutes or less | No added minutes that week; drop grade by one or more ladder levels for the next 2 sessions |
| Flag F3 | 4/10 or more during; OR next-morning stiffness or pain lasting more than 15 minutes; OR pain rising across 3 sessions | Tendon Protocol |
| Stop | 6/10 or more, sharp or pinching pain, a "pop", visible swelling or a thickened tender lump on the tendon, limping, night pain; pinpoint pain on a shin, foot or heel bone that builds as you walk or hurts to press; back pain travelling below the glute, numbness or tingling | Stop incline and loaded leg work; get evaluated by a physician or sports physical therapist |

#### Tendon Protocol

Run this from the day the Flag F3 row applies. Do not wait for Sunday.

1. For 7 days, walk at 5% grade or less. Keep the scheduled minutes while the walk stays at 3/10 or less. If it reaches 4/10 or more on the flat, or you limp, stop for the day and treat it as the Stop row.
2. If the pain is in the calf or Achilles, replace calf raises with isometric holds: 5 × 30–45 seconds, mid-range, daily. That includes the calf work on Legs A and Legs B. For knee or lower-back pain, calf work stays as written and the lift that hurts takes a lighter load or its substitute (Section 4.7).
3. Keep lifting everything that is pain-free.
4. Make no calorie change. This is a loading problem, not a fuel problem. F3 still counts toward your Sunday flag total (Section 1.5).
5. Return in steps: Level 2 (10%), then Level 3 (12%), then Level 5 (15%), with 2 pain-free sessions (Green row) at each step. Never go past the level you worked at before the protocol; if that was Level 0 or 1, go straight back to it. Calf raises come back by step 6.
6. Calf raises return in three leg-day steps after the 7 days, each one only while your pain log is Green. First leg day: 2 sets at about half your last load, heel no lower than the forefoot, no pause. Second: full sets at about three-quarters load, same range. Third: full load, full stretch and the 2-s pause. Keep the daily holds until then.
7. If it is not clearly better after 7–10 days, treat it as the Stop row and get it evaluated.

#### Lower back

| What you feel | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A muscle "pump" that fades within 10 minutes of stepping off | Fatigue, not injury | Shorten your stride, re-brace, drop a ladder level by grade |
| 4/10 or more during; stiffness or pain over 15 minutes next morning; or pain rising across 3 sessions | Flag F3 | Tendon Protocol. The grade drop is the part that unloads your back |
| Pain travelling below the glute, numbness or tingling | Stop | Stop incline and loaded leg work; get evaluated |

### 1.5 Red Flag Biofeedback Checklist

> **Rule:** Score all six flags every Sunday from the week's log. The flag count sets the action. No flag ever lowers your calories.

A deficit of about 1,000–1,100 kcal/day works only while recovery keeps pace. These flags are the earliest measurable signs that it is not, and each has a fixed response. Measurement methods are in Section 6.1; the full Sunday sequence is in Section 6.4.

#### The six flags

| Flag | Name | Trips when |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Sleep | Average under 6.5 hours/night for the week, OR 3 or more nights with trouble falling or staying asleep (awake more than 30 minutes, or waking hungry) |
| F2 | Strength | On 2 or more of the 6 indicator lifts: the first working set is 2 or more reps under your best of the previous 3 sessions at that load, 2 sessions in a row. Example: 10, then 8, then 8 trips it. Deload weeks are not scored |
| F3 | Tendon/joint | Achilles, calf, heel, foot, shin, knee or lower-back pain 4/10 or more during training; OR next-morning stiffness or pain over 15 minutes; OR pain rising across 3 sessions |
| F4 | Hunger | Hunger 8/10 or higher for most of the day on 4 or more of 7 days, OR any binge or near-binge episode |
| F5 | Energy/recovery | Daytime energy 4/10 or lower on 4 or more of 7 days, OR morning resting heart rate 8+ bpm above baseline for 5+ days |
| F6 | Losing too fast | 7-day average dropping more than 1.0% of body weight per week for 2 consecutive weeks (more than about 2.7 lb at 270, 2.5 at 250, 2.3 at 230). Ignore the first week after a diet break |

The 6 indicator lifts are Smith Machine Incline Press, Lat Pulldown, Leg Press, Shoulder Press Machine, Seated Row Machine and Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift. Deload weeks are not scored for F2: compare the week after a deload with the last week before it.

#### Decision rules

| Status | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| GREEN | 0 flags | Run the schedule as written |
| YELLOW | Exactly 1 flag (F1, F2, F4 or F5), first week | No calorie change. Apply the matching targeted fix for 7 days, re-check next Sunday |
| TENDON | F3 (alone or with others) | Tendon Protocol (Section 1.4). A cardio and lifting change, not a calorie change. F3 still counts toward the flag total |
| ORANGE | 2 flags in the same week; OR the same single flag 2 Sundays in a row; OR F6; OR any binge | **Add 200 kcal/day (+50 g carbs around training) for 14 days** |
| RED | 3 or more flags in the same week; OR still ORANGE after 14 days at +200; OR F2 tripping on 3 or more indicator lifts | **7-day maintenance diet break + deload starting the next morning** (tier kcal + 600, deload lifting, flat walking only) |
| MEDICAL STOP | Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, palpitations at rest, shortness of breath out of proportion; any Stop row in the pain scale (Section 1.4); persistent dizziness on standing; any other sign on the Section 1.8 list | Stop training and get medical care. Not a diet decision |

Apply the most severe status that fits: MEDICAL STOP (Section 1.8), then RED, then ORANGE, then YELLOW, then GREEN. TENDON runs alongside any of them whenever F3 is tripped.

Four cases that get misread:

- F3 alone is TENDON only, and calories stay put. F3 plus one other flag is 2 flags: ORANGE plus the Tendon Protocol.
- F3 alone on two Sundays running is ORANGE by the table. It also means the tendon is not clearly better after a week of the protocol: give it until day 10 at most, then treat it as the Stop row.
- F6 is ORANGE on its own. It already took 2 weeks to trip.
- F2 on 3 or more indicator lifts is RED on its own.

#### ORANGE: the +200 rule

Start Monday. Add 50 g of carbs per day, placed around training (the pre-workout and post-workout meals). Protein and fat do not change. Hold it for 14 days. Score the flags as usual on the Sunday in between: if RED applies, it takes over at once; otherwise carry on to day 14.

| At day 14 | Then |
|---|---|
| Flags cleared AND loss still 1.5 lb/week or more | Keep the +200. That is your real intake level: better fuelled at the same result |
| Flags cleared AND loss under 1.5 lb/week | Return to the tier number |
| Flags not cleared | RED |

On Tier 5, read 1.25 lb/week where this table says 1.5.

#### RED: the 7-day maintenance diet break

Start the next morning. Eat tier kcal + 600 with protein at 200 g, fat at 80 g and the rest as carbs. Lift on the deload rules in Section 4.5. Walk flat only, as in a scheduled deload week: 30 min Mon–Fri, no incline above 5%.

How an emergency break fits around the scheduled ones (Weeks 6, 13 and 20, then 26 and 33 in Phase 2):

1. After the 7 days, resume the deficit at the same tier, unless that Sunday's flag check gives a status of its own. Score F1, F3, F4 and F5 as usual (F2 and F6 cannot be scored in a deload week). If RED applies again, take a second maintenance week and book a doctor's visit (Section 1.8).
2. If the emergency break lands within 3 weeks before a scheduled break, it replaces that scheduled break. Run that week as a deficit week.
3. A RED break is never delayed, shortened or skipped, however recent the last break was. Only scheduled breaks move, by rule 2.
4. Your first week back re-enters incline 5 min shorter than your last peak, as after any deload.
5. A second RED within 8 weeks means the deficit is too large for you right now. After that break, eat your ORANGE number (tier + 200 kcal, table below) until the next scheduled break, and book a doctor's visit (Section 1.8).

#### Targeted fixes

|---|---|

| F3 Tendon/joint | Tendon Protocol (Section 1.4) |

Run the fix for 7 days at YELLOW, and keep it running through ORANGE.

#### Too slow: the plateau rule

Trigger: 7-day average loss under 1.0 lb/week for 2 consecutive deficit weeks with 0 flags in both. These weeks do not count: a diet-break week and the week after it; any week with a flag tripped (the flag rules go first, and no flag ever lowers your calories); the first 4 weeks after you start creatine (Section 1.7).

1. Run a one-week tracking audit: food scale on everything, including oils, sauces, drinks and weekends.
2. If loss is still under 1.0 lb/week, drop one tier early: −100 kcal (−16 g carbs, −4 g fat). On Tier 5 that step is 2,000 kcal at 215 / 159 / 56.
3. If you are already at the 2,000 kcal floor, add 5 min per incline session (cap 40) or 1,500 steps/day instead.
4. If you are still stalled 2 more weeks later, take a diet-break week, then reassess.

> **Rule:** Never go below 2,000 kcal/day. Protein never drops.

#### On-track band

| 7-day average loss | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Over 1.0% of body weight per week, 2 weeks running | F6 | ORANGE |
| 1.75–2.25 lb/week | Ideal | None |
| 1.5–1.75 lb/week | Acceptable | No change |
| 1.25–1.75 lb/week on Tier 5 | Ideal for Tier 5 | None |
| Under 1.0 lb/week, 2 consecutive deficit weeks | Plateau | Plateau rule |

Anything else (1.0–1.5 lb/week, a single slow week, a single fast week) trips nothing. Hold the plan and read the next Sunday.

#### Worked examples

**Example A: ORANGE.** Tier 2, 2,200 kcal, 7-day average 250 lb.

1. Sunday log: sleep averaged 6.2 hours (F1). Hunger 8/10 for most of the day on 5 of 7 days (F4). Strength held, no pain, energy fine, loss 2.0 lb (0.8%).
2. Count: 2 flags in the same week. Status ORANGE.
3. From Monday: 2,400 kcal for 14 days. Carbs go from 196 g to 246 g; protein 210 g and fat 64 g stay. The F1 and F4 fixes keep running.
4. Day 14: both flags are clear and the 7-day average fell 3.4 lb across the 14 days (1.7 lb/week). That is 1.5 or more, so 2,400 kcal stays.

**Example B: RED.** Tier 2, 2,200 kcal, Sunday of Week 11.

1. Sunday log: Leg Press and Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift were each 2 reps under their recent best at the same load for the second session running (F2). Energy 4/10 or lower on 5 days (F5). Awake more than 30 minutes on 3 nights (F1).
2. Count: 3 flags. Status RED.
3. From Monday morning (Week 12): 7 days at 2,800 kcal (200 g protein, 320 g carbs, 80 g fat), deload lifting, flat walking only.
4. Sunday of Week 12: the flags have cleared, so Week 13 goes back to 2,200 kcal. The scheduled Week 13 break was within 3 weeks, so the emergency break replaced it. Week 13 runs as a deficit week with incline at 30 min (5 min under the 35-min peak). The Week 20 break stands.

### 1.6 Hydration and Electrolytes

> **Rule:** Keep the gallon (128 oz, about 3.8 L per day) and match it with sodium: 3,000–4,000 mg/day total, food plus added, if your blood pressure is normal.

You already drink the water. Sodium needs checking in both directions. Six sweaty sessions a week remove it and a gallon of plain water dilutes what is left. But the sample days in Section 5.4 are not low-salt: tortillas, sauces, salsa, pickles, cheese and the seasoning blends put them at roughly 5,000–5,600 mg as written (estimate from typical labels). The Sodium Check in Section 5.4 lists the swaps that bring them to about 3,400–3,700 mg. So measure before you supplement. Your logging app totals sodium: read the daily number for your first 2 weeks, and again whenever your menu changes.

| Logged sodium, daily average | What you do |
|---|---|
| Under 3,000 mg | Add one electrolyte serving in training |
| 3,000–4,000 mg | Nothing. Use an electrolyte serving only when a session leaves you drenched |
| Over 4,000 mg | No electrolyte serving. Make the swaps in the Sodium Check (Section 5.4) and add no table salt |

| Water | Number |
|---|---|
| Baseline | 1 gallon (128 oz) per day |
| Sweaty training | Add 16–24 oz per hour on top, if thirsty |
| Hourly ceiling | Never more than about 32 oz (1 L) in an hour |
| Daily ceiling | About 1.5 gallons, sweaty days included. More water is not better |
| Front-load | 3/4 of it (96 oz) finished by 6 pm |
| Urine color target | Pale yellow |

| Electrolyte | Daily target | How to hit it |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium | 3,000–4,000 mg total (food + added) | Check the app total first. Add an electrolyte serving in training only if you log under 3,000 mg |
| Potassium | About 3,400 mg, from food | Potatoes, bananas, beans, yogurt, spinach, tomato products, salmon. No high-dose potassium pills |
| Magnesium | About 400–420 mg total | If food falls short: 200–350 mg of elemental magnesium (as glycinate) in the evening. 350 mg/day is the upper limit from all supplements combined, so count the multivitamin and the electrolyte mix |
| Calcium | About 1,000–1,300 mg | 2–3 servings of dairy |

> **Rule:** If you have high blood pressure or have been told to limit salt, stay at or under 2,300 mg sodium and follow your doctor.

**Daily timing.** Built for late-afternoon or evening training.

| When | Fluid | Electrolytes and notes |
|---|---|---|
| On waking | 16–20 oz water | Plain water |
| Breakfast to lunch | About 32 oz | Sodium comes with the food. Add none to a Section 5 meal |
| Lunch to pre-workout meal | About 24–32 oz | Sodium comes with the food |
| Lifting + incline session | 24–32 oz | One zero-sugar electrolyte serving (about 300–500 mg sodium) mixed in, only if your logged sodium is under 3,000 mg |
| Post-workout meal, evening | The rest of the gallon | Sodium comes with the food. Magnesium glycinate here, if you use it |
| Last 2 hours before bed | Taper to sips | Keeps bathroom trips from breaking your sleep |

Checkpoint: 96 oz down by 6 pm. If you train after 6 pm, reach 96 oz before you walk in and let the session bottle be the last quarter.

**Low-electrolyte signs:** headache, muscle cramps, light-headedness on standing, unusual fatigue, crystal-clear urine all day.

> **Rule:** Fix low-electrolyte signs with an electrolyte serving. Do not add more plain water.

Check this before you blame the calories: the same signs can trip flag F5 (Section 1.5). If your logged sodium is already over 3,000 mg, they are probably not a sodium problem: score F5 as usual. Dizziness on standing that persists after the fix belongs in Section 1.8.

### 1.8 Medical and Safety Note

This manual is education, not medical care. At 18, with a starting BMI near 38 and a deficit that runs for most of a year, get a baseline physical before Week 1 if you can and within the first 2–3 weeks if you cannot, and tell the doctor what the plan is. Until then, get your blood pressure read on two different days, before any caffeine or training. Use an upper-arm cuff that fits: measure around the middle of your upper arm and check it against the range printed on the cuff. A cuff that is too small reads falsely high, by 5–20 points on a large arm, and many pharmacy kiosks have one size. If your arm is over the range, ask the pharmacist for a large or extra-large cuff. Sit for 5 minutes first, back supported, feet flat, arm resting at heart height, no talking. Take 2 readings a minute apart each day and average all 4.

| Average reading | What you do until a doctor has seen you |
|---|---|
| Under 130 and under 80 | Run the plan as written |
| 130–139 or 80–89 | 2,300 mg sodium limit (Section 1.6). No pre-training caffeine. Train as written. Visit within 2–3 weeks |
| 140 or higher, or 90 or higher | 2,300 mg sodium limit. No caffeine. Flat walks only. Every lifting set at 3–4 RIR, breathing out on the effort. Visit within a week |
| 180 or higher, or 120 or higher; or any high reading with severe headache, chest pain, vision change or breathlessness | Do not train. Get medical care the same day |

| Bring up at the visit | Why it matters here |
|---|---|
| Blood pressure | It decides your sodium target (Section 1.6) and matters before months of vigorous incline work |
| Basic blood panel | A baseline to compare against later. Your doctor chooses the markers |
| Heart history | Tell the doctor about any fainting or chest pain during exercise, and any close relative with heart disease or sudden death before 50. It changes what should be checked before vigorous work |
| Sleep | Loud snoring, pauses in breathing or heavy daytime sleepiness point to sleep apnea, which is common at this BMI. It trips flags F1 and F5 whatever you eat |
| The plan itself | 2,300 kcal stepping down to a 2,000 kcal floor, 210 g protein, about 2 lb/week, 6 lifting days, incline walking at about 9.5 METs |
| Medications, conditions, supplements | List all of them. Creatine can nudge the creatinine reading on a blood test, so say you take it |

**Follow-up.** Book a second visit for the May 9 checkpoint, before Phase 2 starts: weight, blood pressure and a repeat of the blood panel. Bring the Master Log (Section 6.3).

> **Rule:** Any sign below means stop training and get medical care. It is not a diet decision, and no calorie change fixes it.

1. Chest pain or pressure.
2. Fainting or near-fainting.
3. Palpitations at rest.
4. Shortness of breath out of proportion to the effort.
5. Persistent dizziness on standing.
6. Stop-level tendon or joint pain (Section 1.4): 6/10 or more, sharp or pinching pain, a "pop", visible swelling or a thickened tender lump on the tendon, limping, night pain, or pinpoint pain on a shin, foot or heel bone that builds as you walk or hurts to press.
7. Back pain travelling below the glute, or numbness or tingling.
8. Dark, cola-colored urine, or muscle pain, swelling and weakness far beyond normal soreness, in the 1–3 days after a hard session.
9. Steady pain in the upper right or upper middle of your abdomen lasting more than 30 minutes, often after a meal, with or without nausea or pain under the right shoulder blade.
10. One calf swollen, warm, red or tender when the other is not.
11. New numbness between your legs, loss of bladder or bowel control, or a leg or foot going weak.

Signs 1–4 need medical care the same day; if one is severe or does not settle within minutes of stopping, treat it as an emergency. Sign 5 needs a medical visit before you train again. Signs 6 and 7 mean no incline and no loaded leg work until a physician or sports physical therapist has evaluated you. Signs 8, 10 and 11 need medical care the same day: do not wait for a clinic slot. Sign 9 needs a visit within a few days, and the same day if it comes with fever, yellow skin or eyes, or lasts more than a few hours.

Everything short of this list is handled by the weekly flags in Section 1.5. Those change calories, cardio or lifting; this list ends the session.

**Sick days.** Illness is the one thing that cancels training. A fever of 100.4°F or more, body aches, a chest cough, vomiting or diarrhea means no gym until you have been fever-free for 24 hours. On those days aim for your diet-break calories, eat what you can and do not make it up later. The full rule, including how to come back, is in Section 4.6. Chest pain, palpitations or unusual breathlessness when you return is on the stop list above.

Last point: a plan this strict can start running your head. Signs to take seriously:

- Binges, or eating in secret.
- Making up for food: skipped meals, days under target or under the floor, extra cardio, vomiting or laxatives.
- Dread or guilt about the maintenance weeks, the +200 rule or a meal you could not weigh.
- Turning down meals with family or friends because of the scale and the app.
- Weighing more than once a day, or a morning number that decides your mood.
- Wanting to go under the floor or past the finish line.

If any of these shows up in most weeks of a month: eat at your diet-break calories for 7 days, keep lifting, and tell someone you trust. If it does not settle, or if vomiting or laxatives are ever involved, stay at maintenance and see your doctor or a registered dietitian before you cut again. That is a health matter, not a discipline failure. Before Week 1, tell one person at home what the plan is, including the 2,000 kcal floor and the 200 lb finish line.

### 2.2 How to Read the Tables

- **Target 7-day avg** is the model's projection for the average of your seven morning weigh-ins that week (Monday to Sunday). The baseline is a 7-day average of 270.0 lb for Nov 9–15. If your baseline is different, shift the whole column by the difference. If you only have a single Nov 16 weigh-in, expect to sit about 1 lb above the column all the way down; judge the weekly change, not the offset.
- **On-track range** is the corridor between losing 2.25 lb and losing 1.75 lb in every deficit week so far. Inside the corridor, change nothing. It widens over time because small weekly differences add up. Diet-break weeks carry 2 lb of extra headroom because the scale normally rises 1–3 lb from water, glycogen and food volume.
- **The scale overrides the calendar.** Tier changes happen when your 7-day average actually crosses 255, 240, 225 or 210 lb (Section 3), not on the dates projected here. Cross early and you change early; cross late and you stay on the higher tier until you get there. The three diet-break weeks are fixed by date unless a RED status moves one (Section 1.5).
- **Incline walk** means 15% grade at 3.0 mph with your hands off the rails. Minutes are minutes at the working grade; add a 3-minute ramp-up and a 3-minute ramp-down. If you cannot hold it hands-free under the 160 bpm ceiling, use the Incline Ladder in Section 1.4. The minutes stay the same at any ladder level.
- **Flat walk** means 0–3% grade at 3.0 mph, conversational pace. It follows both leg days.
- **Weekly layout never changes:** Mon Push A, Tue Pull A, Wed Legs A, Thu Push B, Fri Pull B, Sat Legs B, Sun rest. Cardio always comes after lifting. Sunday has no required cardio; an easy 20–30 minute walk is optional. The step floor is 8,000 steps every day.
- **Lifting focus** uses the week types defined in Section 4.5 (Baseline or Re-entry, Progression, Peak, Deload). RIR is reps in reserve.
- **P / C / F** is grams of protein, carbohydrate and fat. Every row satisfies kcal = 4 × P + 4 × C + 9 × F.
- **Gym closed for a holiday** (Thanksgiving is Thu Nov 26, Christmas Day is Fri Dec 25, New Year's Day is Fri Jan 1): that day becomes your rest day and each remaining session of the week moves one day later, ending Sunday (Section 4.2). Never stack two lifting sessions in one day.

## 3. Weight-Milestone Adjustment Triggers

Four body weights change your protocol: 255, 240, 225 and 210 lb. Each one resets calories, carbs, fat and incline minutes to fit the smaller body doing the work. The scale pulls the trigger, not the calendar.

### 3.1 How the Triggers Work

> **Rule:** A milestone is hit the first time your 7-day average is at or below it. A single weigh-in never counts. The new protocol starts the following Monday.

The 7-day average (Section 6.1) filters out water, sodium and food volume, so the plan only changes for real tissue loss.

1. **Confirm on Sunday, switch on Monday.** Calories, macros and incline minutes change together (Section 6.4).
2. **Never during a diet-break week.** Scheduled or emergency: the new tier starts the Monday after the break.
3. **Never while ORANGE or RED.** Hold the change until the status clears (Sections 1.5 and 6.4).
4. **Never two tier drops within 3 weeks.** A plateau step counts as a drop.
5. **Data overrides the calendar.** The dates below are projections (Sections 2.3 and 7.3). Cross early, switch early. Cross late, switch late.
6. **Incline rises 5 min at a time.** If a re-entry week or the Tendon Protocol leaves you more than 5 min short of the new number, add 5 min per session each week.

What never changes at any milestone:

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| Protein | 210 g/day at every tier (215 g in Tier 5). It never drops |
| Calorie floor | 2,000 kcal/day. Nothing in this plan goes below it |
| What a step down removes | 16 g carbs and 4 g fat (100 kcal). Never protein |
| Lifting | Same six workouts, same 6 indicator lifts, same double progression (Section 4) |
| Incline standard | 15% at 3.0 mph, hands off the rails. If you need the rails, drop a ladder level (Section 1.4) |
| Incline limits | 40 min per session, 4 sessions per week, never on a leg day |
| Step floor | 8,000 steps/day, treadmill steps included |

## 4. Planet Fitness 6-Day PPL & Cardio Integration Plan

Lifting has one job in a deficit: make sure the weight you lose is fat, not muscle. Exercises, order, sets, reps and rests are fixed.

### 4.1 Program Rules

> **Rule:** Every working set is logged, stops at the prescribed RIR, and uses the same range of motion as last week. A shortened, bounced or twisted rep does not count.

**RIR (reps in reserve)** is how many more clean reps you had left when you stopped. Anchor: if 12 full Lat Pulldown reps to the collarbone is your true limit at a load, RIR 2 means stopping at rep 10.

| RIR | The last rep looks like | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 4–5 | As fast as the first rep | Deload weeks |
| 2–3 | First clearly slower rep | Compounds, most weeks |
| 1 | Slow but clean | Isolations; compounds in Peak weeks |
| 0 | Another full rep would fail | Last set of machine or cable isolations, Peak weeks only |

If your last rep moved as fast as your first, that was RIR 4–5, not 2.

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 2–3 s lowering, no bounce |
| Range | Full pain-free range; same depth and seat setting every week |
| Failure | 0 RIR only where the table above allows. Never on Smith Machine Squat, Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift, Leg Press or any dumbbell press (no spotter) |
| Rest | As listed, timed. Short rests cost reps and fake a strength loss |
| Session length | 55–70 min lifting, then cardio. Whole visit: about 1.5 hours in Week 1, about 2 hours at 40-min incline |

**Warm-up, every session:**

1. 5 min easy flat treadmill or bike.
2. Leg days: 6 bird-dogs per side, 6 dead bugs per side, 20-second side plank per side. Side plank from the knees until 20 seconds with straight legs is easy.
3. First exercise: 2–3 ramp-up sets at about 50% × 8, 70% × 5 and 85% × 2–3 of the working load. For a 120 lb pulldown: 60 × 8, 80 × 5, 100 × 2–3 (nearest pin).
4. Second exercise: 1 ramp-up set at about 70% × 5. Ramp-up sets are not logged.

**Logging:**

- Load × reps for every working set; seat and pad settings once.
- Smith machine bars are counterbalanced and their starting weight varies by club and by machine. Log the plates per side, not a guessed total, and always use the same machine.
- The first working set of each indicator lift goes on the Section 6.2 sheet.

### 4.2 Weekly Schedule

> **Rule:** The day order never changes. Incline is never done on a leg day and never runs more than 2 days in a row (Mon–Tue, then Thu–Fri).

| Tue | Pull A (lat focus) | Incline walk |

### 4.4 Double Progression

> **Rule:** Reps go up first, then load. The load changes only when every set reaches the top of the rep range at the target RIR.

1. Start with a load that lands in the bottom-to-middle of the rep range at the target RIR.
2. Keep that load until every set reaches the top of the range at the target RIR.
3. Next session, add the smallest available load (table below).
4. Reps fall back toward the bottom of the range. Rebuild.
5. Big-jump rule: where the next jump is more than about 10%, or the rep range is only 2 reps wide (10–12), first earn the top of the range + 2 reps on every set, then jump.
6. Split-jump rule: where the next jump is more than about 20% (dumbbells under 30 lb per hand, the lightest cable and machine pins), first earn the top of the range + 4 reps on every set. Then take the new load on set 1 only and keep the old load on the other sets. Move set 2 up once set 1 reaches the bottom of the range, then set 3. If a split jump fails twice, swap that slot at the next block start (Section 4.7).
7. Failed jump: if set 1 at the new load cannot reach the bottom of the range (split jump: 2 reps under the bottom), finish that session at the old load. Stay there until every set is 2 reps higher than it was before the attempt, then try again. Each failed attempt adds 2 more.
8. After a jump, sets 2 and 3 may land 1–2 reps under the bottom of the range. That is not a failed jump: keep the load and rebuild.
9. Only full-range, controlled reps count.

| Equipment | Smallest jump |
|---|---|
| Smith machine, plate-loaded | +5 lb total (2.5 lb per side); +10 lb total if only 5s are available |
| Selectorized machines and cables | The 5 lb add-on weight where the stack has one (a dial, lever or slider at the top of the stack); otherwise +1 plate/pin. Log the add-on with the pin |
| Dumbbells | +5 lb per hand |

Jump check = jump ÷ current load. A stack going 120 → 130 lb is 8%: normal rule. Dumbbells going 40 → 45 lb is 12.5%: big-jump rule. Dumbbells going 15 → 20 lb is 33%: split-jump rule.

Worked example: Lat Pulldown, 3 × 8–12 at RIR 2, 10 lb pins, one session per week.

| Session | Load | Set 1 | Set 2 | Set 3 | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 lb | 10 | 9 | 8 | In range. Keep the load |
| 2 | 120 lb | 11 | 10 | 9 | Reps up. Keep the load |
| 3 | 120 lb | 12 | 11 | 10 | Only set 1 is at the top. Keep the load |
| 4 | 120 lb | 12 | 12 | 12 | Every set at the top. Add 1 pin next session |
| 5 | 130 lb | 9 | 8 | 8 | Reps fell back, as expected. Rebuild to 3 × 12 |

Had set 1 at 130 lb stopped at 7 or fewer, step 7 applies. Late in a block, one added rep on one set is progress.

**Finding your loads in Week 1.** For each exercise, pick a load you are sure you could lift 15 times. Do the bottom number of the rep range. If the last rep was as fast as the first, rest 90 s, add one step (one pin, 10 lb on the Smith machine, 5 lb per hand) and repeat. Stop adding when the last rep is the first clearly slower rep. That is your Week 1 load: do your working sets there. At most 4 feeler sets; if you run out, use the last load and adjust next week. On the Smith machine lifts the first feeler set is the empty bar. Feeler sets are not logged and replace the ramp-up sets in Week 1.

### 4.5 Stalls, Deloads and RIR by Week Type

> **Rule:** A stall is no added rep on any set for 3 consecutive sessions of the same lift while biofeedback is otherwise green. Keep the load and match the logbook. An accessory may be swapped for its listed substitute at the next block start. An indicator lift is never swapped and never lightened to restart progress.

Sitting 2 or more reps under your recent best at the same load, 2 sessions in a row, on 2 or more indicator lifts is not a stall. It is flag F2 (Section 1.5).

- Holding load × reps while your body weight falls about 2 lb a week is the job done. A lighter load would hide the strength loss flag F2 exists to catch.
- Deload and re-entry weeks do not count toward the 3 sessions.
- The only reset: at a load you were already repping inside the range, set 1 falls below the bottom of the range 2 sessions in a row. Drop about 10% (nearest pin, plate or dumbbell), rebuild, write "reset" on the Section 6.2 strength check and mark the trend down.

| Week type | Weeks | Sets | Compounds RIR | Isolations RIR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline / Re-entry | 1; 7, 14, 21; 27, 34 | As written | 2–3 | 2 |
| Progression | 2–4, 8–11, 15–18, 22–24; 28–31, 35–38 | As written | As in Section 4.3 | As in Section 4.3 |
| Peak | 5, 12, 19, 25; 32, 39 | As written | 1–2 | Last set of machine or cable isolations 0–1; rest as written |
| Deload | 6, 13, 20; 26, 33 | 2 (1–2 where 2 are written) | 4–5 | 4–5 |

- Compounds = chest, shoulder and leg presses, rows, pulldowns that bend the elbow, squats and Romanian deadlifts. Everything else is an isolation, including Cable Straight-Arm Pulldown, triceps pushdowns, calf presses and Cable Pallof Press.
- Week 1 sits at the cautious end (about 3 RIR on everything) while you find loads. Re-entry weeks use the last pre-deload loads for full sets.
- Calf and trunk work keep their written RIR and tempo in every non-deload week.
- Squats and Romanian deadlifts of any kind, substitutes included, never go below 2 RIR, Peak weeks included.
- Weeks 26 onward are Phase 2 (Section 7.3). Weeks 40–41, the run-in to the finish line, are strength defense: match the logbook, compounds at 2 RIR, no grinders.
- Strength defense in Weeks 18 and 22–24 is a Progression week with a pass mark: match last week's load × reps on all 6 indicator lifts. A lift that reaches the top of its range on every set still takes its jump.
- Where the weekly focus line in Section 2.4 is more specific than this table, follow it.

**Deload prescription (Weeks 6, 13 and 20; Weeks 26 and 33 in Phase 2; R1 of the reverse diet):**

| Item | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Exercises | Same six sessions, same exercises, same order |
| Sets | 2 instead of 3; 1–2 instead of 2 |
| Load | Same as the week before the deload |
| Reps | Stop 4–5 short of failure: about 3–4 fewer per set than last week |
| Cardio | No incline above 5%. Flat walk 30 min Mon–Fri |

It should feel easy. Do not add sets or test strength. An emergency (RED) break uses the same prescription (Section 1.5).

### 4.6 Cardio Integration

> **Rule:** Lift first, walk second, same visit. If you must split them, the walk can come any time later that day. A walk done before lifting must end at least 6 hours earlier. Incline is never done on a leg day.

Lifting decides how much muscle you keep, and it needs fresh legs and a fresh lower back. Walking does not.

**Incline-day order:** last lifting set, prehab, 3-min ramp-up (1 min each at 5%, 10%, 12%), the prescribed minutes at 15% and 3.0 mph (or your ladder level) hands-free, 3-min ramp-down, calf stretch. Ramps do not count. Form, ladder, heart-rate and tendon rules: Section 1.4.

| Day | Walk | Why it sits there |
|---|---|---|
| Mon, Tue | Incline | Upper-body days leave the legs fresh. Two days in a row is the limit |
| Wed | Flat (0–3%, 3.0 mph) | Legs A already loaded calves, quads and lower back |
| Thu, Fri | Incline (Fri flat in Weeks 1–3) | Friday is held back so you build from 3 incline days to 4 |
| Sat | Flat (0–3%, 3.0 mph) | Legs B fatigued the hamstrings and lower back that hold your lean on a 15% grade |
| Sun | Optional easy walk | Unloads the Achilles before Mon–Tue |

**Step floor:** 8,000 steps every day, treadmill steps included. A 30-min walk at 3.0 mph is 1.5 miles, roughly 3,000–3,500 steps (estimate); the rest comes from your day. It is a floor, not a score to beat: no bonus cardio.

**Short on time:**

> **Rule:** Lifting is never the thing that gets cut.

1. Do the full lifting session with full rests on the compounds.
2. Trim the flat walk first and make up the steps later that day. It costs least: about 80–100 kcal for 20 min, against about 465 kcal for 30 min of incline at 270 lb (planning estimates).
3. On an incline day, come back later the same day for the walk. If you cannot, shorten the working minutes, keep the prehab and both ramps, and log the minutes done.
4. Missed minutes are not made up. Never add them to another session, and never put incline on a leg day or a rest day.

**Missed or sick days:**

> **Rule:** A missed session is never doubled up. Fever, or any symptom below the neck: no gym.

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| One training day missed, Mon–Sat | That day becomes your rest day. Move that session and each later session of the week one day later, ending Sunday. Order unchanged; each session keeps its own walk |
| Two or more days missed in a week | Do not catch up. Resume with the session the calendar shows and log the gap |
| Cold symptoms above the neck only, no fever | Train on the deload prescription (Section 4.5). Flat walk instead of incline |
| Fever, chest cough, body aches, vomiting or diarrhea | No training. Return after 24 hours with no fever and the other symptoms clearly fading |
| First 2 sessions back after a fever, or the first session back after 3 or more days off | Last loads, 2 sets, 3–4 RIR, flat walk. Then incline restarts 5 min under your last session |

On a fever day, aim for your diet-break calories and eat what you can (Section 1.8). A week with 2 or more missed days does not count toward the plateau rule (Section 1.5).

### 4.7 Accessory Swaps and Busy-Gym Substitutes

A **substitute** is a one-day replacement from the Section 4.3 tables. A **swap** changes an accessory for the rest of a block.

> **Rule:** Accessory swaps are allowed only in the first week of a block (Weeks 7, 14 and 21; Weeks 27 and 34 in Phase 2). The 6 indicator lifts never change, so the strength log stays comparable from Week 1 to the finish line.

Swap only for a reason: a stall, a joint that keeps complaining, or missing equipment. The new exercise keeps the slot's sets, reps, RIR and rest and restarts double progression at step 1. Any Section 4.3 substitute can become the swap, plus these:

| Movement pattern | Extra swap options |
|---|---|
| Press | Smith Machine Flat Press; Smith Machine Seated Shoulder Press |
| Fly, lateral raise | Low-to-high Cable Fly; Seated Dumbbell Lateral Raise |
| Triceps | Straight-bar pushdown |
| Row, lat accessory | Seated cable row; underhand pulldown |
| Rear delt, biceps | Dumbbell reverse fly; any dumbbell, cable or machine curl |
| Squat pattern | Goblet Squat; Smith Machine Split Squat (Legs B only, from 240 lb) |
| Leg curl, leg extension | Single-leg versions of the same machine |
| Calves | Equipment only. One straight-knee and one bent-knee lift always stay, at the same tempo |

**The 240 lb unlock.** Once your 7-day average is at or below 240.0 lb (Section 3), Smith Machine Split Squat may replace Leg Press, feet high and shoulder-width, on Legs B. It is optional and goes in at the next block start, like any swap (Week 21 on the default track). Same slot: 3 × 10–15 per side, RIR 2, 2.5 min rest, never to failure. Why wait: a split stance puts most of your body weight plus the bar through one knee and hip, and that costs less 30 lb lighter. Set-up: front heel flat, back foot on its ball, drop straight down until the back knee is an inch off the floor. First session: empty bar only. If that is not good for 10 clean reps per side at RIR 2, keep the Leg Press.

**When a machine is taken:**

1. Two sets or fewer left for the person on it: wait.
2. More: do the next exercise, then come back. Keep the indicator lift within the first two exercises.
3. Still taken: use the PF substitute that day and log it under its own name.
4. Indicator lift substituted, or a different Smith machine used: write "sub" on the Section 6.2 strength check and compare next week against your last session on the real lift.

- **Missing from your club:** the substitute becomes your default from Week 1. If it replaces an indicator lift, it is your indicator for the whole plan.
- **Joint pain of 3/10 or more** that a lighter load does not fix (pain scale: Section 1.4): use the substitute that day. If it returns next session, keep the substitute and make it a swap at the next block start.
- **Dumbbells stop at 75 lb.** If the 75s reach the top of a range, swap to the Smith or machine version at the next block start.
- **Stack maxed on a machine.** Keep the full stack and make every rep harder, in this order: a 4-s lowering, then a 1-s pause at the bottom, then a top of range 3 reps higher. Log each change once. This applies to Leg Press too: write the change on the Section 6.2 strength check and compare only weeks done the same way. Calf work goes to one leg at a time instead.

### 4.8 Set-Up Notes for the Six Indicator Lifts

> **Rule:** Before every Smith machine set, set the safety stops and test them with the empty bar. Squats, Romanian Deadlift and calf raises: just below the bottom of the rep. Presses: the lowest setting that still holds the bar off your chest and neck when you relax on the bench. Same machine, bench spot and seat number every week. On slanted rails, face the same way every session.

**Smith Machine Incline Press (Push A)**

- Bench at 30°: the low incline setting, clearly below 45°.
- Bar path: position the bench so the bar lowers to your upper chest, just below the collarbones. On slanted rails it should travel up and back toward your face.
- Grip just outside shoulder width, forearms vertical at the bottom, elbows 45–60° from your ribs, shoulder blades back and down.
- Stops: lie on the bench, lower the empty bar onto the stops and breathe out. The bar must rest just above your chest, never on it and never over your neck. If the nearest setting leaves the bar 1–2 in off your chest, that height is your bottom position every week. Log the setting.
- A rep that stalls: lower the bar to the stops or roll it onto the nearest hook. Do not grind it.

**Lat Pulldown (Pull A)**

- Thigh pad snug; overhand grip just outside shoulder width.
- Torso: lean back 10–15° and hold that angle all set.
- Shoulder blades down first, then elbows toward your back pockets. Bar to the collarbone, never behind the neck.

**Leg Press (Legs A)**

- Seat set so your knees reach about 90° at the bottom with hips and lower back flat on the pad. Log the seat number.
- Feet mid-platform, shoulder width or slightly wider, toes out 15–30°, so your thighs travel beside your torso, not into it.
- Depth ends the moment your tailbone starts to curl off the pad. Hips stay down: a pelvis that tucks under load is how the leg press hurts lower backs.
- Knees track over the toes; stop short of locking them.

**Shoulder Press Machine (Push B)**

- Seat height: handles start just above shoulder height, about chin level, forearms vertical. Handles below the shoulders means the seat is too high.
- Back on the pad, ribs down. Arching loads the lower back.
- Elbows slightly in front of your body; use the neutral handles if the wide ones pinch.

**Seated Row Machine (Pull B)**

- Chest pad set so you can only just reach the handles with straight arms; handles level with your lower chest.
- Chest stays on the pad every rep. If it lifts off, the load is too heavy and your lower back is doing the work.
- Elbow path: back and down, close to the ribs, finishing at the hip line.

**Smith Machine Romanian Deadlift (Legs B)**

- Rack the bar at mid-thigh so you start standing tall. Feet hip-width.
- Hinge: unlock the knees 15–20°, hold that bend, push the hips straight back.
- Bar path: down the thighs, never more than an inch from your legs. On slanted rails, face the way that brings the bar toward your shins as it lowers. Check with the empty bar: if the bar drifts away from your legs as you hinge, turn around.
- Range: stop when your hips stop moving back, usually between kneecap and mid-shin. Lower than that is rounding, not range.
- Neutral spine: ribs down, brace, chin tucked. The set ends the moment your back position changes.

**Smith Machine Squat (Legs A)**

- Bar on the upper traps. Feet shoulder width, 4–6 in ahead of the bar line, toes out 15–30°. Further forward makes the pelvis tuck at the bottom, and the lower back pays.
- Sit straight down; knees track over the toes; heels stay flat.
- On slanted rails the bar moves about 3 in forward or back between top and bottom. Measure the 4–6 in at the bottom of the rep with the empty bar. If you cannot keep heels flat, knees over toes and lower back neutral there, turn around and test again. Then keep that direction and foot spot every week.
- Depth: thighs parallel, or the deepest point where your lower back stays neutral and heels stay down, whichever comes first.

**Calf-raise tempo (Legs A and Legs B)**

- Every rep: 3-s lower, 2-s pause in the full stretch with the heel below the forefoot, controlled rise. No bounce.
- Straight-knee (Legs A): press the platform out with your whole feet first, then slide one foot at a time down until the balls of both feet are on the lower platform edge. The whole ball of each foot stays on the plate, not just the toes. Knees straight but not jammed back.
- Slip protection: on a plate-loaded sled press, leave the safety catches engaged for the whole set. On a seated stack press, set the seat as far back as still lets you reach the full stretch without the weights touching down, so a slip moves the platform only a few inches.
- Full stack too light for 8–12 at RIR 2 on two legs: switch to one leg at a time at about half the load. Same tempo, 3 sets per leg, 60 s between legs. Log it as single-leg from then on.
- Bent-knee (Legs B): flat bench inside the Smith machine, knees at 90°, forefeet on one or two stacked 45 lb plates, high enough that your heels do not touch the floor at the bottom. Bar across your lower thighs, 2–3 in back from the kneecaps, on a bar pad or a folded towel or mat. Set the hooks so you lift the bar off by rising onto your toes, and set the stops just below your full-stretch position.
- Why: the pause removes the elastic rebound, so calf and Achilles work under slow, heavy load, which builds tolerance for the 15% grade. Straight knee trains the gastrocnemius; bent knee the soleus.
- Pain during calf work: Section 1.4 pain scale.

## 5. High-Protein Meal Plan & Food Playbook

Sections 5.1–5.3 are the building blocks: the eating rules, the food matrix and the desserts. Sections 5.4–5.7 turn them into full days, tier scaling, a grocery list and a prep routine.

### 5.1 How to Eat on This Plan

> **Rule:** Every day, land within 10 g of your protein target and within 50 kcal of your calorie target (Section 2.3).

Protein, together with the lifting, keeps the loss coming from fat instead of muscle. Calories set how fast you lose. Every other rule here exists to make those two numbers easy to hit and hard to fake.

**When a day goes off target.** In 41 weeks it will. Log what you ate as honestly as you can and go back to the normal target at the next meal. Do not eat less the next day, skip a meal or add cardio to pay for it: a day 500 kcal over costs about 0.15 lb and the 7-day average absorbs it. A day under target is not banked for later either. Two or more days over in one week is hunger data: check flag F4 on Sunday (Section 1.5). A meal you cannot weigh (restaurant, family dinner, holiday): build the plate protein-first, log your best estimate, move on. On Tier 4 the ±50 band is 2,000–2,050 kcal: the floor wins.

| Non-negotiable | Number | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | Target ±10 g, every day | Holds muscle in a deficit; the most filling macro |
| Calories | Target ±50 kcal, every day | Sets the rate of loss. Exercise calories are never eaten back |
| Carbs and fat | Trade inside the calories; fat stays at 50 g or more | Food choice without fat running too low |
| Food scale | Everything in grams; meat raw unless a row says cooked | Eyeballed portions run high |
| Meals | 4 meals + 1 dessert or snack | Fewer decisions, steadier hunger |
| Protein per meal | 40–55 g at each of the 4 meals | Hits the target without one giant dinner |
| Training meals | Pre-workout meal 60–120 min before; post-workout meal within about 2 hours after | Most of the day's carbs go in these two meals, where they fuel the lifting and the incline walk |
| Fiber | 30–40 g/day from food | Fullness on fewer calories |
| Produce | A vegetable or fruit at every meal | Volume and potassium for few calories |
| Logging | One app, the same saved entries, logged before you eat | Comparable numbers week to week |
| Drinks | Liquid calories only if they carry protein | Juice, regular soda and sweet coffee drinks do not fill you |

Zero-calorie drinks cost nothing: water, zero-sugar soda, black coffee, plain tea. Water and caffeine limits are in Sections 1.6 and 1.7.

**Weigh and log the same way every time:**

1. Scale on the counter, set to grams. Bowl on, tare, add the food, read.
2. Meat and fish: raw weight against a raw entry. When you batch-cook, weigh the batch raw, weigh it again cooked, and portion by that ratio. Estimate: 6 oz raw chicken breast cooks down to about 4.5 oz.
3. Rice: cooked weight. Pasta and oats: dry weight. Potatoes: raw weight.
4. Oil, cooking spray, peanut butter, cheese and sauces are measured every time. They are where untracked calories hide.
5. Build each meal once in the app, save it, and reuse the saved meal. Never switch entries for the same food.

#### The Plate Formula

> **Rule:** 1 protein + 1 carb + 1 fat + sauce or seasoning + free vegetables = one meal of 450–600 kcal with 45–55 g protein.

|---|---|---|
| Protein | One meat or fish row, or two rows from eggs, dairy and powder | A meat or fish row carries 29–39 g protein, except canned tuna and deli turkey (22 g): add one egg, dairy or powder row to either. | Carb | One row | Largest portions go in the pre-workout and post-workout meals |

1. Blend or shake the base for 30 seconds. Pour it into the pint container, stay under the max-fill line, and freeze it level for 24 hours.
2. Spin on Lite Ice Cream. If it comes out crumbly, add 1 tbsp milk and Re-spin, up to twice.

Without one (blender and freezer; works for all three pints):

1. Pour the same base into ice-cube trays and freeze at least 4 hours. If the cubes will wait more than a day, tip them into a freezer bag.
2. Let the cubes stand 5–10 minutes. **Protein Root-Beer Float** (180 kcal, 30.5 g protein, 9 g carbs, 2.5 g fat)

1 scoop (31 g) vanilla whey, 1/2 cup (120 ml) ultra-filtered fat-free milk, 1 can (355 ml) zero-sugar root beer, cold, 2 tbsp (9 g) light whipped topping, 4 ice cubes.

3. Add the whipped topping. Eat the foam with a spoon, drink the rest.

#### Storage and Reheating

> **Rule:** Sauce goes on after reheating. Anything crisp goes back in the air fryer, never the microwave.

| Food | Store | Reheat |
|---|---|---|

| Sweet potato wedges, roasted vegetables | Shallow box | Air fryer, 400°F, 3–4 min |
| Rice | Flat and sealed; first into the fridge | 1 tbsp water, cover, microwave 60–90 s |
| Taco turkey, chicken cubes, smash patties | Sealed box | Covered, microwave 60–90 s; sauce after |
| Scramble wrap | Foil. Eggs, spinach and cheese inside; no salsa | Unwrap. Microwave 90 s, turning once, then dry pan 1–2 min per side until crisp and steaming hot in the middle |

| Romaine, slaw mix, spinach | Dry, with a paper towel | Never |
| Avocado | Whole until the day; lime juice on the cut face | Never |

Food-safety basics:

1. **Two hours.** Cooked food is in the fridge within 2 hours of leaving the heat. Use shallow containers and leave the lids off until the steam stops.
2. **Four days.** Refrigerated leftovers are good for 3–4 days at 40°F or colder. This schedule never holds anything longer than 3. Label every container with the day and the meal.
3. **Steaming.** Reheat until steaming hot all the way through (165°F), and reheat only once.
4. **Raw meat.** Cook or freeze raw chicken, ground meat, fish and shrimp within 1–2 days of buying them, and steak within 3–5 days. Thaw in the fridge, on a plate on the bottom shelf, never on the counter, and cook within 1–2 days of thawing.
5. **Separate.** One board and one set of tongs for raw meat. Wash hands, board and counter before you touch cooked food.
6. **Away from home.** A packed meal rides in an insulated bag with two ice packs, or goes into a fridge when you arrive. Cooked food gets 2 hours in total outside a fridge, 1 hour in a hot car. Microwave it until steaming. No microwave there: pack that day's cold meal instead (Monday and Thursday the chicken sandwich with the cutlet cold, Tuesday and Friday the overnight oats, Wednesday and Saturday the Buffalo wrap, rolled that morning and not toasted) and eat the meal it replaces at home. The order of the meals does not change the day's numbers.

| Metric | How | When |

### 6.4 Sunday Decision Procedure

Ten minutes, same order every Sunday.

1. **Average.** Compute the 7-day average weight, the change from last week in lb, and the change as a percent of body weight.
2. **Milestone.** If the average is at or below 255, 240, 225 or 210 lb for the first time, the new tier in Section 3 starts tomorrow, with two exceptions: if tomorrow starts a diet-break week, apply the new tier the Monday after; if today's status comes out ORANGE or RED, hold the tier change until the status clears.
3. **Flags.** Score F1 to F6 against the week's log (Section 1.5). Count them. Note whether any flag also tripped last Sunday.
4. **Rate.** Loss above 1.0% of body weight for the second week running trips F6. Loss under 1.0 lb for the second deficit week running, with 0 flags in both weeks, starts the plateau rule. Skip this step in a diet-break week and in the first week after one.
5. **Status.** Apply the highest status that fits: MEDICAL STOP, then RED, then ORANGE, then YELLOW, then GREEN. TENDON runs alongside any of them whenever F3 is tripped.
6. **Write it down.** Tick the decision, write next week's calories, macros and incline prescription at the bottom of the sheet, and update the Master Log row.
7. **Look ahead.** Read next week's row in Section 2.3 and Section 2.4 (Section 7.3 from Week 26) so Monday starts with no open questions.
8. **Finish line (Phase 2 only).** If the 7-day average is at or below 200.0 lb, or the waist has measured 35.5 in or less on two consecutive Mondays and you are satisfied with the relaxed side-on photo, the cut is over (Section 7.2). The reverse diet in Section 7.4 starts tomorrow.

> **Rule:** One decision per week on calories and the schedule, made on Sunday, from the 7-day average and the flag count. No mid-week changes because of a single weigh-in, a bad session or a good mood. Pain and illness never wait for Sunday: the pain scale and the Tendon Protocol apply from the day they are triggered (Section 1.4), and any sign on the Section 1.8 list stops training on the spot.

### 7.4 The 4-Week Reverse Diet

> **Rule:** The reverse starts the Monday after the finish line and runs 4 weeks. Intake rises 150 kcal every Monday, weighed and tracked exactly like the cut. Protein settles at 200 g. Added calories go to carbs first and fat second, with fat capped at 80 g. There is no free-eating week.

| The reverse does | The reverse does not |
|---|---|
| Raise food in controlled steps, which lowers the risk of a rebound binge | "Repair" your metabolism |
| Find maintenance from your own data | Freeze the scale at the finish number |
| Refill glycogen so training volume can rise | Add fat while intake is still under maintenance |
| Taper incline volume and keep the habit | End tracking or weigh-ins |

### 7.6 Guardrails: How to Not Regain

> **Rule:** During R1–R4 your calories only hold or rise. No rule in the reverse lowers them.

Estimated true maintenance with the tapered cardio is about 2,700–2,900 kcal at 200 lb. R4 sits at or just under it. Fat regain needs a sustained surplus. On these estimates (±250 kcal, Section 1.2) the schedule contains none; the hold rule and the flat test check that against your scale. If you finish heavier, maintenance is higher (about 2,900–3,100 kcal at 224 lb), so expect more steps after R4.

1. **Keep the Sunday check-in.** Weigh daily and score the flags through the reverse and for at least 8 weeks after (Section 6.4). F3 still means the Tendon Protocol. ORANGE adds its 200 kcal on top of the scheduled week, and the +200 stays until the flat test. During the reverse the floor guard replaces flag F6. RED means go straight to the R4 intake the next morning, or keep your current intake if it is higher, and stay there, with 7 days of deload lifting and flat walking only.
2. **Floor guard.** If the 7-day average on the R1, R2 or R3 Sunday is more than 1.0 lb below the previous Sunday's, take a double step the next Monday: +300 kcal instead of +150, all of it carbs (75 g). Example: after R1 at 2,250 kcal, R2 becomes 2,550 kcal at 200 / 289 / 66 instead of 2,400. Then return to +150 kcal steps by the Section 7.4 method. The reverse still ends after R4. Reason: the early weeks are still well under maintenance, and the plan does not want you drifting far below 200 lb.
3. **Hold rule.** Your end-of-cut low is the 7-day average on the finish Sunday. Your hold line is that low + 4 lb. Above the hold line on 2 consecutive Sundays: hold calories for one week. Do not cut. Then continue.
4. **Track everything until maintenance is confirmed.** Same tolerances: calories within ±50 kcal, protein within ±10 g. If hunger spikes, run the F4 fix (Section 1.5). If a flag raises your target, raise it on paper and hit it. Never eat past it untracked. Repeated binges or dread of eating more: Section 1.8.
5. **Flat test.** After R4, hold your intake and watch the next two Sundays. If both 7-day averages sit within 1 lb of the R4 Sunday average, that intake is maintenance. If one falls more than 1 lb below it, add 100–150 kcal on Monday (25 g carbs per 100 kcal), take that Sunday as the new reference and start the two-Sunday count again. If the average rises more than 1 lb per week for 2 weeks, go back one step.

| What the Sunday 7-day average does | What you do |
|---|---|
| R1–R2: down 0.5–1 lb from last Sunday | Expected. Take the scheduled +150 kcal on Monday |
| R1–R2: 1–2 lb under the end-of-cut low | Expected. It usually levels in R3–R4 |
| R1–R3: more than 1.0 lb below last Sunday | Floor guard: +300 kcal on Monday instead of that Monday's usual step |
| Above the end-of-cut low but under the hold line | Mostly water, glycogen and food volume. Stay on schedule |
| Above the hold line on 1 Sunday | Stay on schedule. Check tracking accuracy. Re-check next Sunday |
| Above the hold line on 2 consecutive Sundays | Hold calories for 1 week. Do not cut. Then continue |
| After R4: more than 1 lb below the reference Sunday | Add 100–150 kcal on Monday. Restart the two-Sunday count |
| After R4: two Sundays in a row within 1 lb of the reference Sunday | Stop adding. That intake is maintenance |
| After R4: rising more than 1 lb per week for 2 weeks | Go back one step. That intake is maintenance |

### 7.7 After the Reverse: Maintenance and Building

> **Rule:** Hold maintenance for at least 8 weeks. The next phase is building muscle, not another cut. This plan does not cut below 200 lb.

With a beginner's muscle mass at 200 lb, you improve your look far more by adding muscle than by going lighter.

1. **Find maintenance.** Run the flat test (Section 7.6). From R4 on the default track, expect 0–2 more steps (estimate).
2. **Hold it for at least 8 weeks.** The hold starts once the flat test passes, and the two flat-test weeks count as its first two. On the default track the reverse ends Sun Sep 26, so the hold runs to about late November 2027. Daily weigh-ins, full tracking and the Sunday check-in continue for all 8 weeks. Keep the R4 cardio and the 4-set indicator lifts. The next deload is due 6–7 weeks after R1.
3. **Then build.** Eat at measured maintenance or a small surplus of 150–250 kcal, with protein at 200 g. Training and cardio follow the table below.

**If the time cap ended your cut.** Run the same reverse and hold maintenance for at least 8 weeks before any further cut. Then re-enter at the tier for your current weight (Section 3) under the same rules.

### Double Progression in Four Lines (Section 4.4)

1. Same load until every set hits the top of the rep range at the target RIR.
2. Then add the smallest jump available and rebuild from the bottom of the range.
3. No added rep for 3 sessions with everything else green: keep the load and match the logbook. Reset about 10% only if set 1 falls below the rep range 2 sessions in a row.
4. Only full-range, controlled reps count.
