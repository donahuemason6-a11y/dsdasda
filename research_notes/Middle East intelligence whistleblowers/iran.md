## Key Question 1: Mehdi Hashemi and the Ash-Shiraa leak (1986-1987)

### Takeaway
Mehdi Hashemi, a cleric, former IRGC officer and head of the "Global Islamic Movements"/liberation-movements apparatus run from Ayatollah Montazeri's office, is the long-standing consensus source (via his followers) of the 3 November 1986 Ash-Shiraa story exposing McFarlane's Tehran trip; he was arrested in October 1986, confessed on television, was tried for three days by the Special Court for the Clergy in August 1987 and shot at dawn on 28 September 1987 for "corruption on earth." Journalists and scholars call him a radical factional operator and "leaker," not a whistleblower, and both his exact role in the leak and the torture behind his confession remain disputed (a CIA analysis even argues Syria was the actual leaker).

### Cited Findings
- Hashemi was a Shia cleric who later held a senior Revolutionary Guards post; he had been jailed by the Shah's SAVAK and was freed when the monarchy collapsed in 1979; he was later defrocked by the Special Clerical Court and executed in 1987 — [Wikipedia, "Mehdi Hashemi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi)
- AP (28 Sept 1987) described him as having "headed the Global Islamic Movement, which was in charge of exporting the Islamic revolution" until his arrest "last October" (October 1986), and as having run Montazeri's office in Qom — [AP report, "Former Head Of Islamic Movement Executed," via ECU digital library](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf)
- He was "brother of Ayatollah Montazeri's son-in-law" and "himself a senior officer of the Revolutionary Guard"; he "ran an organization from Montazeri's office that aimed to export the revolution" and "is thought to have embarrassed Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani by leaking information of his connection with the Iran-Contra affair"; he "was accused of being the source that informed a Lebanese newspaper (al-Shiraa) of a secret visit by a U.S. delegation to Tehran to meet with Rafsanjani" — [Wikipedia, "Hussein-Ali Montazeri"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein-Ali_Montazeri)
- The Ash-Shiraa story appeared in the magazine's 3 November 1986 issue; "it was only because of Ash-Shiraa's account of former National Security Advisor Robert McFarlane's visit to Tehran that the story made global headlines"; the magazine is described as pro-Syrian and editor Hassan Sabra wrote the unsigned cover story — [Wikipedia, "Ash-Shiraa"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash-Shiraa); [Daniel Pipes, "Breaking the Iran-Contra Story"](https://www.danielpipes.org/13852/breaking-the-iran-contra-story); [American Heritage](https://www.americanheritage.com/content/ash-shiraa-first-reports-iran-contra-affair)
- McFarlane's trip: Walsh's Iran-Contra report records that McFarlane "remained involved in the Iran weapons sales, acting as President Reagan's emissary on a mission to Tehran in May 1986"; a CSM chronology dates the flight to 28 May 1986, "accompanied by NSC staff member North and three other men. Their plane carries military spare parts" — [Walsh Report, ch. 1 (FAS)](https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/chap_01.htm); [CSM, "Key events in the Iran-contra affair"](https://www.csmonitor.com/1987/0227/achron.html)
- Motive as reported: Hashemi "apparently leaked it to discredit Rafsanjani, making the disclosure in hopes of discrediting Rafsanjani by exposing his contacts with the Americans"; "the Montazeri faction leaked embarrassing information about Rafsanjani's dealings with the United States" — [ExecutedToday, "Mehdi Hashemi"](https://www.executedtoday.com/tag/mehdi-hashemi/)
- Arrest: "Hashemi was arrested just before the story broke publicly" (i.e., October 1986) — [ExecutedToday](https://www.executedtoday.com/tag/mehdi-hashemi/); AP: "Until his arrest last October" — [AP via ECU](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf)
- Mehdi Karrubi dates the Montazeri-Khomeini rift to "around October 1986 when Montazeri sent a letter to the latter, criticising and questioning the foundation of the state" — [Wikipedia, "Hussein-Ali Montazeri"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein-Ali_Montazeri)
- Televised confession: before trial he "appeared on television and admitted involvement in subversive activities"; one commentary describes the broadcast as a Soviet-style self-denunciation extracted after "75 lashes" (single-source, unverified) — [ExecutedToday](https://www.executedtoday.com/tag/mehdi-hashemi/). Wikipedia notes: "Evidence that Hashemi was tortured to confess comes from an unsympathetic source" — [Wikipedia, "Mehdi Hashemi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi)
- Trial: "Mehdi Hashemi was convicted by a religious court in a three-day trial in August [1987]"; the Special Court for Clergy "only deals with charges against clerics" — [AP via ECU](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf)
- Charges: convicted of being "corrupt on earth, the most serious crime under the Islamic Shariaa"; Tehran radio also listed "murder, kidnapping, plotting to overthrow the government and smuggling arms" — [AP via ECU](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf). Wikipedia lists the Special Clerical Court charges as "sowing corruption on earth, inciting fitna, succumbing to Satan, and desecrating the martyrs of the Islamic Revolution," and says per prosecutor Reyshahri the accusations also included opium smuggling and attempting to kill a Montazeri rival; Reyshahri "took the opportunity to deny the 'insidious notion' that Hashemi was being punished because of his opposition to the McFarlane visit" — [Wikipedia, "Mehdi Hashemi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi)
- Execution: AP, Monday 28 September 1987, citing Iranian state radio: "A relative of Ayatollah Hussein Ali Montazeri... was executed by a firing squad in Tehran at dawn today"; "The death sentence... was carried out today at 6 a.m." — [AP via ECU](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf)
- ExecutedToday: "Hashemi was secretly executed … seemingly, for leaking the Iran-Contra scandal" (note conflict: AP says state radio announced it) — [ExecutedToday](https://www.executedtoday.com/tag/mehdi-hashemi/)
- Conflicting date: a UNHCR/Refworld chronology says he was executed in 1986 after being sentenced for "plotting against the Islamic regime" — contradicted by AP and Wikipedia (Sept 1987) — [UNHCR chronology PDF](https://webarchive.archive.unhcr.org/20230526072903if_/https://www.refworld.org/pdfid/415193854.pdf)
- A 1976 murder case (of cleric Shamsabadi) is referenced in Wikipedia's account of Hashemi's pre-revolution record, but I could not read the passage (fetch blocked) — [Wikipedia, "Mehdi Hashemi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi)
- Scholarly treatment of the leak: a Yale Review of International Studies essay ("Importing Arms, Exporting the Revolution: Mehdi Hashemi and His Fatal Leak to Ash-Shiraa") notes "factual points of disagreement regarding how and when Hashemi and his followers came to know of McFarlane's visit as well as how and why Hashemi was arrested," and argues the leak "must be interpreted as a retaliation for Hashemi's arrest" — [YRIS](https://yris.yira.org/essays/importing-arms-exporting-the-revolution-mehdi-hashemi-and-his-fatal-leak-to-ash-shiraa/)
- National Security Archive's Malcolm Byrne: "for most of the last 28 years, the general consensus has been that the perpetrators were supporters of a notorious Iranian radical named Mehdi Hashemi," but "a few small mysteries remain. One of them is who leaked the original story" — [Unredacted/National Security Archive (2014)](https://unredacted.com/2014/11/04/iran-contra-who-leaked-ronald-reagans-1985-1986-arms-for-hostages-deals/)
- Competing account: a declassified CIA analysis argues Damascus leaked the deal; "the Syrian Government first learned of the arms-for-hostages deal from its chargé in Tehran, Iyad Mahmud, who probably got the information through his contacts in the Iranian Government"; Syria "apparently learned of the story in early October"; "a group of Iranian officials kidnaped Mahmud and beat him badly before letting him go" — [CIA Reading Room, "How the Iran-Contra Story Leaked"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/node/1822411)
- Rafsanjani's response in Tehran: he "described in some detail a secret mission made by McFarlane and four other US officials to Tehran, and moved quickly to make it appear as though Iran had duped the Great Satan" — [Daniel Pipes](https://www.danielpipes.org/13852/breaking-the-iran-contra-story)
- Official Iranian narrative today: the regime's oral-history site presents "the crimes of Mehdi Hashemi Gang" — [oral-history.ir](https://www.oral-history.ir/print.php?id=9735)
- Effect on Montazeri: Khomeini's dismissal letter (dated 26 March 1989 in most accounts; "not made public at the time and was published a few years later, so some have questioned the letter's validity") told Montazeri to stop charity funds going "to Mehdi Hashemi's circle and to liberals," and said Khomeini had opposed his selection because of his "weakness and foolishness" — [Iran Data Portal, "Letter Dismissing Montazeri"](https://irandataportal.syr.edu/letter-dismissing-montazeri/); [Wikipedia, "Deputy Supreme Leader of Iran"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Supreme_Leader_of_Iran)
- Ahmad Khomeini's 1989 letter to Montazeri "questioned his affection for Hashemi"; Montazeri defended Hashemi — [Wikipedia, "Ahmad Khomeini"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Khomeini)
- The 1989 crisis also followed Montazeri's private letters criticizing the 1988 prison executions, whose contents VOA broadcast — [CSM, 31 March 1989, "Radicals Hold Sway in Tehran"](https://www.csmonitor.com/1989/0331/omont.html)
- Montazeri's son says Rafsanjani "cried and begged that the March 26 letter not be read out on radio"; the Assembly of Experts endorsed the dismissal on 4 June 1989, the day after Khomeini's death; Montazeri lost the Grand Ayatollah title, state-radio mention and his security guards — [IranWire](https://iranwire.com/en/features/61942); [Wikipedia, "Deputy Supreme Leader of Iran"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Supreme_Leader_of_Iran)
- Related 1986 context: Montazeri wrote to Khomeini in 1986 "informing him that the IRGC was involved in the plot" (explosives in Hajj pilgrims' luggage); sources disagree whether Hashemi was blamed for or uninvolved in that plot — [Radio Farda](https://en.radiofarda.com/a/29538233.html); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/irans-revolutionary-guard-smuggled-suitcases-of-explosives-into-saudi-arabia/)

### Inferences
- The documented core is: Hashemi's position, the factional motive against Rafsanjani, the October 1986 arrest, the November 1986 publication, the televised confession, the August 1987 clerical-court trial and the 28 September 1987 execution. The disputed elements are (a) whether Hashemi personally leaked or his followers did so after his arrest (Byrne, YRIS), (b) whether Syria rather than Hashemi's circle was the proximate source (CIA analysis), (c) whether the confession was tortured (single hostile source), and (d) whether the "real" charges were the leak or the murder/smuggling counts (Reyshahri's denial suggests the regime was sensitive to the former).
- No source treats Hashemi as a "whistleblower"; the vocabulary is "leak," "radical," "gang" (regime) or "factional retaliation" (scholars). His aim was to sabotage a policy, not expose wrongdoing to the public on principle, which is why the whistleblower label is not applied.
- The case is the clearest early example of the Islamic Republic treating an internal leak about security policy as a capital, religiously framed crime ("corruption on earth"), tried in a special court outside the ordinary judiciary.

### Gaps
- Exact day of the October 1986 arrest and exact broadcast date of the televised confession (commonly given as December 1986) could not be verified from retrievable sources.
- I could not access the Tower Commission or Walsh report text on the leak's source; Byrne's summary suggests neither names the leaker.
- The Abdorrahman Boroumand Center memorial database has entries for Tehran executions on 28 September 1987, but the excerpts did not name the victims, so I could not confirm a dedicated Hashemi record ([iranrights.org date index](https://www.iranrights.org/memorial/browse/date/222)).
- The YRIS essay's full argument and the Shamsabadi 1976 case details were not readable (fetch blocked).

## Key Question 2: Abolghasem Mesbahi ("Witness C"), the Mykonos trial and the AMIA case

### Takeaway
Mesbahi, a senior VEVAK/MOIS officer who had served in Paris and Bonn, was briefly jailed in 1988 as a suspected US double agent and defected in 1996 into German police protection; as "Witness C" his testimony was described as "crucial" to the Berlin Kammergericht's 10 April 1997 Mykonos verdict, which found Iran's top leadership ordered the 1992 killings and triggered the EU's recall of ambassadors and the end of its "critical dialogue." His later AMIA testimony in Argentina (1998, 2000, 2002) was used by prosecutor Nisman but is widely disputed, with US analysts reportedly calling him a "serial fabricator"; he is universally described as a "defector" or "witness," not a whistleblower.

### Cited Findings
- The killings: on 17 September 1992, eight Iranian opposition figures, four of them Kurdish, were dining at the Mykonos restaurant in Berlin "when two men walked in, opened fire at the table, and killed four of the eight men" — [Grove Atlantic, "Assassins of the Turquoise Palace"](https://groveatlantic.com/book/assassins-of-the-turquoise-palace/); Amnesty refers to "the September 1992 killings of three KDPI leaders and an interpreter in Berlin" — [Amnesty International, 10 April 1997](https://www.amnesty.org/ar/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mde130151997en.pdf)
- Mesbahi's background: "a former senior official in Iran's security service, Vevak, and was based in, among other places, Paris and Bonn"; "In late 1988 he was imprisoned briefly as a suspected US double agent and in 1996 defected" — [Megrahi You Are My Jury (review of Al Jazeera Lockerbie documentary)](https://www.megrahiyouaremyjury.net/?p=1031)
- His Argentine deposition says he "defected from the Iranian secret service in 1996 and placed himself under German protection" — [Consortium News (Gareth Porter)](https://consortiumnews.com/?p=9573); German reporting says he "lives in Germany under BKA protection" — [taz](https://taz.de/Raetseln-ueber-Iran-Connection/!1097774/)
- Identity: the US State Department's Patterns of Global Terrorism 1996 says Bani-Sadr "also referred to a suspected former Iranian intelligence officer (so-called Witness C and later identified as Abolqasem Messbahi), who also was called to testify" — [Refworld, Patterns of Global Terrorism 1996 - Germany](https://www.refworld.org/node/44411)
- "He said he had first-hand knowledge of the plot, and his testimony proved crucial in the subsequent trial of some of the Iranians" — [Megrahi You Are My Jury](https://www.megrahiyouaremyjury.net/?p=1031)
- A Wilson Center summary of a talk (based on Hakakian's research) describes a defector who was "one of Fallahian's deputies," who "defected in late 1996," "testified and backed up the prosecution's other witnesses," but whose "other allegations are not entirely accurate" — [Wilson Center, "Justice Iranian Style"](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/event/justice-iranian-style-tehrans-extrajudicial-assassinations)
- Página/12 (2002): the Germans regard him as "un arrepentido valioso" (a valuable turncoat) because he helped clarify the Mykonos killings — [Página/12, 4 Dec 2002](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/diario/elpais/1-13771-2002-12-04.html?mobile=1)
- Trial scale: presiding judge Frithjof Kubsch "presided over 246 sessions and heard the testimony of 166 witnesses"; the trial ran about three and a half years (opened October 1993); "guilty verdicts for four of the accused were announced in April 1997" — [Roya Hakakian](https://royahakakian.com/?p=539); [Mideast Freedom Forum](https://www.mideastfreedomforum.org/index.php?L=1&id=459)
- Verdict, 10 April 1997: the court "attributed the killings to Iran's political leadership" with the motive "to silence an uncomfortable voice"; it "ruled that the murders had been ordered by Iran's Committee for Special Operations to which Ayatollah Rafsanjani, Iran's president at the time, and Ayatollah Khamenei, the spiritual leader, belonged" — [NY Sun, "Dial M for Democracy"](https://www.nysun.com/article/opinion-dial-m-for-democracy); the court "concluded that the Mykonos murders had been approved at the most senior levels of the Iranian Government by an extra-legal committee whose members included the Minister of Intelligence and Security, the Foreign Minister, the President, and the Supreme Leader" — [Refworld, Patterns of Global Terrorism 1997 - Germany](https://www.refworld.org/node/43850)
- Sentences: Amnesty reported four convicted, "the fifth -- a Lebanese -- was acquitted"; "Kazem Darabi, an Iranian said to have organized the killings for the Iranian secret service, and Abbas Rhayel, accused of firing the fatal shots, were given life sentences" — [Amnesty International, 10 April 1997](https://www.amnesty.org/ar/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mde130151997en.pdf). (A Federal Lawyer article says two received 23-year terms; Amnesty's contemporaneous account is preferred — [Federal Lawyer, May/June 2014](https://www.fedbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/legalcomm-mayjun14-pdf-1.pdf))
- Fallahian: "An international arrest warrant was issued for intelligence minister Ali Fallahian"; "Before the trial began, Fallahian reportedly flew to Berlin to meet with his German counterpart, offering to set free a number of refugees in exchange for ending the trial" — [NY Sun](https://www.nysun.com/article/opinion-dial-m-for-democracy); the court declared the assassination "had been ordered by him with knowledge of Khamenei and Rafsanjani" — [Wikipedia, "Mykonos restaurant assassinations"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos_restaurant_assassinations)
- Evidence limits: a 1995 German government answer says it had not sent records requested by the Kammergericht and investigative committee, and witness-testimony permissions were granted only narrowly — [Bundestag Drucksache 13/3393](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/13/033/1303393.pdf)
- EU response: the EU statement said the Mykonos findings established "the involvement of the Iranian authorities at the highest level," called this "totally unacceptable in the conduct of international affairs," noted the critical dialogue "had been agreed upon at the European Council in December 1992," invited Member States "to recall their ambassadors for coordinated consultation," and concluded "there is no basis for the continuation of the critical dialogue" — [UN document A/52/120 (EU statement)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/233280/files/A_52_120-EN.pdf)
- "All member states of the European Union withdrew their ambassadors, the first time such an event had occurred since 1979"; Canada also withdrew its ambassador — [Wilson Center](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/event/justice-iranian-style-tehrans-extrajudicial-assassinations)
- Timing: the verdict "came weeks before Iran's presidential elections, at a time when the polls showed the moderate candidate, Khatami, behind the hard-line Nateq Nouri" — [Roya Hakakian, US News piece](https://royahakakian.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/US-News-World-Report.pdf)
- Later: "the convicted killers Darabi and Rhayel are released in 1997 [sic] and deported back to Iran and Lebanon respectively" (the year given appears erroneous; their release was later) — [Kurdistan Memory Programme](https://kurdistanmemoryprogramme.com/assassination-of-iranian-kdpi-leaders/)
- AMIA testimony: "testigo C" testified in Germany in April 1998 and in Mexico in May 2000; in Karlsruhe (2002) "he ratified in general what he had said in the earlier statements, in 1998 and 2000" — [Página/12, 13 Nov 2004](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/diario/elpais/1-43562-2004-11-13.html); [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-8393-c-ratifico-sus-dichos-anteriores)
- He alleged Menem's government "had negotiated impunity for the 1994 attack in exchange for $10 million" deposited in a Swiss account; he later reportedly changed which account the money came from — [Página/12, 13 Nov 2004](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/diario/elpais/1-43562-2004-11-13.html); a Uruguayan outlet citing the New York Times says the money went "into a numbered account that Menem designated" — [Montevideo Portal](https://www.montevideo.com.uy/Archivo/INCULPAN-A-MENEM-EN-EL-ATENTADO-A-LA-AMIA-EN-ARGENTINA-HACE-UNOS-ANOS-uc356)
- Nisman relied on him: "Nisman also cited the testimony of Abolghasem Mesbahi, who called himself a 'defector' from the Iranian intelligence service, that Iranian officials had made such a decision sometime in August 1993"; Nisman's 2006 Interpol request said Mesbahi "is a qualified witness" "because of his background within VEVAK" — [Lobelog (Gareth Porter)](https://lobelog.com/the-nisman-murder-and-the-amia-bombing-a-tangled-thread/)
- Nisman's roughly 900-page 2006 warrant request against seven senior Iranians centered on an alleged 14 August 1993 meeting; Judge Rodolfo Canicoba Corral issued arrest warrants in November 2006; Interpol upheld six Red Notices in November 2007 — [Consortium News, 7 Feb 2015](https://consortiumnews.com/2015/02/07/a-rush-to-judgment-in-argentine-bomb-case/); [S.Res.277 (116th Congress)](https://www.congress.gov/116/bills/sres277/BILLS-116sres277ats.pdf)
- Credibility attacks: Porter reports US intelligence analysts viewed Mesbahi as a "serial fabricator" who "had also told a false story about Iran and the 9/11 attacks"; Mesbahi said by his own account he had left Iranian intelligence in 1985 and "offered no evidence" for the 1993-decision claim; Nisman allegedly omitted that Mesbahi's "secret 100-page deposition" in Mexico in 2000 "claimed the planning for the attack had begun in 1992" — [FPIF, "Argentina's Tangled Web"](https://fpif.org/argentinas-tangled-web/); [Consortium News](https://consortiumnews.com/?p=9573)
- Diario Judicial: "what he has said so far about Menem is vague and could not be confirmed" — [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-8684-el-testigo-que-resulto-una-c); Página/12 reported a bank he named does not exist — [Consortium News](https://consortiumnews.com/?p=9573)
- Counterpoint on reliability: a Spanish-language legal report says details Mesbahi gave in 1998 about Iranian intelligence operations outside the Middle East "were later confirmed" — [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-8684-el-testigo-que-resulto-una-c)
- Lockerbie: a German journalist "believes that the evidence that Mesbahi gave in the Mykonos case was credible, [but] is very sceptical of his claims about Lockerbie. Mesbahi himself acknowledged that all his information about Lockerbie was second-hand" — [Megrahi You Are My Jury](https://www.megrahiyouaremyjury.net/?p=1031)
- The "founder" claim: a Washington Times piece says he "had helped set up Iran's intelligence service, the MOIS, as far back as the mid-1980s" and claims advance warning of 9/11; Consortium News says he had not worked for Iranian intelligence since 1985 by his own account — [Washington Times, 30 Dec 2011](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2011/dec/30/what-now/); [Consortium News](https://consortiumnews.com/?p=9573)
- AMIA judicial outcomes: Menem's cover-up trial was ordered in August 2015; in February 2019 the court acquitted him; Judge Galeano received six years and ex-intelligence chief Anzorreguy four and a half — [Buenos Aires Times](https://batimes.com.ar/news/argentina/judge-who-led-amia-bombing-probe-given-6-years-in-jail-menem-cleared.phtml); [Israel Hayom](https://israelhayom.com/2019/03/01/former-president-of-argentina-cleared-in-cover-up-of-jewish-center-bombing)
- Hakakian's book review notes "details of the defection of Abolghasem Messbahi, the agent from the Ministry of Intelligence who turned into the trial's key witness," reveal "how the ministry operated, including rules imposed on agents" — [Washington Independent Review of Books](https://www.washingtonindependentreviewofbooks.com/bookreview/assassins-of-the-turquoise-palace)

### Inferences
- The Berlin court evidently found his Mykonos testimony credible enough to rely on alongside Bani-Sadr and documentary evidence (the verdict named the leadership committee he described), but no retrievable source quotes the Kammergericht's own credibility assessment; the Hakakian-based summary that his "other allegations are not entirely accurate" suggests even sympathetic chroniclers treat him as a mixed witness.
- The sharp split between German acceptance (Mykonos) and US/critical skepticism (AMIA, Lockerbie, 9/11) is the defining feature of his record: a defector whose first-hand knowledge was valuable but whose later, second-hand claims expanded in ways many observers found opportunistic.
- Terminology: courts and press call him "Witness C," "defector," "key witness," "arrepentido"; he is not framed as a whistleblower because he testified under protection after defecting rather than exposing wrongdoing while inside.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the Kammergericht's written judgment (Urteilsgründe) or the Mykonos summary PDF hosted by the US District Court (SDNY) to quote the court's assessment of Witness C.
- The exact date of the international arrest warrant for Fallahian (commonly given as March 1996) was not confirmed in retrievable text.
- Mesbahi's precise rank/title in VEVAK ("deputy to Fallahian" vs. "number three" vs. "co-founder") is asserted differently across sources and could not be reconciled.

## Key Question 3: The 1998 "chain murders," the Ministry of Intelligence's admission, Saeed Emami, and Akbar Ganji

### Takeaway
After the stabbing of Dariush and Parvaneh Forouhar (22 Nov 1998) and the killings of writers Mohammad Mokhtari and Mohammad Jafar Pouyandeh, Khatami's probe forced the Ministry of Intelligence on 4-5 January 1999 to admit "rogue" agents were responsible; minister Dorri-Najafabadi resigned 10 Feb 1999, deputy minister Saeed Emami was arrested and died in custody in 1999 (officially suicide by depilatory cream), and a closed military trial in January 2001 sentenced 18 agents (life terms and death sentences) in what families called a sham. Journalist Akbar Ganji, a former IRGC member and intelligence-ministry employee, used insider knowledge to implicate Fallahian ("Master Key") and Rafsanjani ("Red Eminence"), was jailed 2000-2006, and is described as an investigative journalist/dissident/prisoner of conscience rather than a formal whistleblower; genuine insider leaks included a three-hour interrogation video of Emami's wife leaked in June 2001 by intelligence agents close to reformists.

### Cited Findings
- Forouhars: "Dariush and Parvaneh Forouhar were stabbed to death in their Tehran home on November 22 [1998]. Both sustained multiple stab wounds" — [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-slain-dissidents-daughter-under-pressure-forouha/28869824.html)
- "Mokhtari's body was discovered on December 3 near a cement factory on a road south of Tehran, several days after he went missing" — [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-slain-dissidents-daughter-under-pressure-forouha/28869824.html)
- The ministry "officially acknowledged responsibility for the killings of Dariush and Parvaneh Forouhar, Mohammad Mokhtari, and Mohammad-Ja'far Pouyandeh" but "denied involvement in other cases, including Pirouz Davani's death" — [Iran International, Dec 2024](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202412063661); [IranWire](https://iranwire.com/features/65674/)
- Admission date and wording: "On January 4, 1999, the ministry issued an unprecedented statement partially acknowledging responsibility by blaming 'rogue' agents for four of the murders" — [Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202412063661); other accounts date it 5 January and say the ministry "announced that the perpetrators of the killings had been 'rogue' members of the ministry" — [IranWire](https://iranwire.com/features/65674/); UANI: "an unprecedented statement in January 1999, acknowledging for the first time its participation in crimes but pinning the blame solely on Emami and his rogue agents" — [UANI, "The Chain Murders"](https://www.unitedagainstnucleariran.com/node/40662)
- Khatami "order[ed] the Intelligence Ministry to release a statement in January 1999, attributing the Chain Murders to a 'group of rogue elements' in the ministry" — [Wikipedia, "Chain murders of Iran"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_murders_of_Iran)

### Inferences
- The chain-murders episode is the only case in which the Islamic Republic itself officially admitted security-service responsibility for political killings; the "rogue agents" formula and Emami's death in custody functioned to cap accountability at deputy-minister level, which Ganji's reporting directly challenged by naming Fallahian and Rafsanjani.
- Ganji fits part of the whistleblower profile (ex-insider using inside knowledge to expose state crimes, punished with "collecting confidential information" charges) but exposed the crimes as a journalist years after leaving the ministry, so sources call him an investigative journalist, dissident or prisoner of conscience; the 2001 charge of possessing a "confidential government bulletin" shows the state treated him as a leaker of classified material.
- The June 2001 interrogation-video leak is the clearest documented case of serving intelligence officers leaking to the reformist side; it derailed the espionage/"sexual misconduct" case against Emami's circle and led to the arrest of the interrogators, showing intra-ministry factional leaking rather than public whistleblowing.

## Key Question 4: Nuclear-program disclosures — NCRI/Natanz (2002), Shahram Amiri (2009-2016), and genuine insiders

### Takeaway
On 14 August 2002 NCRI spokesman Alireza Jafarzadeh revealed the Natanz and Arak sites at a Washington press conference, but analysts such as Jeffrey Lewis show the US had already briefed the IAEA on Natanz and that the NCRI misdescribed its purpose, while Israeli authors and a New Yorker report suggest Western intelligence was "laundered" through the MEK-linked group; the NCRI remained a designated terrorist alias whose Washington office was closed in 2003. Shahram Amiri, a Malek Ashtar/AEOI-linked scientist, vanished in Medina in 2009, resurfaced in the US, returned to Tehran in July 2010 claiming kidnapping, disappeared and was hanged in early August 2016 for giving "confidential and vital" information to the US; no genuine Iranian nuclear insider has publicly blown the whistle on the record.

### Cited Findings
- "On August 14, 2002, at a press conference in Washington, DC, the National Council of Resistance of Iran (NCRI), an exiled Iranian opposition group, drew worldwide attention"; Jafarzadeh "described two 'top secret' nuclear facilities being constructed in Iran at Natanz and Arak under the guise of front companies that procured nuclear materials and equipment" — [NDU WMD Center / Carnegie case study](https://wmdcenter.ndu.edu/Media/News/Article/627151/the-international-atomic-energy-agencys-decision-to-find-iran-in-non-compliance/); [Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/middle-east/posts/2012/12/the-international-atomic-energy-agencys-decision-to-find-iran-in-non-compliance-2002-2006)
- The venue was Washington's Willard Hotel, per an advocacy-leaning anniversary piece — [Lopez/Pundicity](https://lopez.pundicity.com/26400/iran-nuclear-appeasement); RFE/RL's timeline: the exile group "accuses Tehran of hiding a uranium-enrichment facility at Natanz and a heavy-water plant at Arak" — [RFE/RL timeline](https://www.rferl.org/a/1056420.html)

### Inferences
- The Natanz revelation is best characterized as an opposition group publicizing intelligence (whatever its provenance) rather than insider whistleblowing: the NCRI had no inside role in the program, misidentified the site's function, and the IAEA already had the location from the US. The "laundering" thesis is well sourced to named authors but rests on anonymous officials.
- Amiri is treated by Western media as a defector/spy in a "mystery," and by Iran alternately as kidnapping victim, hero and then executed spy; only Iranian state media (ISNA) used the English word "whistleblower," apparently loosely. His execution shows that even a returnee hailed as a hero was later tried in secret and hanged.
- I found no case of an Iranian nuclear insider publicly disclosing program details on principle; the only insiders are recruited agents or defectors handled covertly by foreign services, consistent with the lethal consequences shown in the Amiri case.

## Key Question 5: Reza Kahlili ("A Time to Betray") and Ali Reza Asgari

### Takeaway
"Reza Kahlili" is a pseudonymous former Revolutionary Guard member who says he spied for the CIA in the 1980s-90s; his 2010 memoir won publishing awards and he lectures at the Pentagon's Joint Counterintelligence Training Academy, but he appears masked, a Middle East Quarterly reviewer faulted the book's lack of verifiable detail, Washington Post intelligence reporter Jeff Stein called him "self-proclaimed," and US officials "rolled their eyes" at his claims, with only lawyer Mark Zaid publicly vouching for a CIA relationship. Ali Reza Asgari, an IRGC general, Lebanon/Hezbollah architect and deputy defense minister (1997-2005), vanished in Istanbul in February 2007; the Washington Post reported he was willingly cooperating with Western intelligence, Iran alleged kidnapping, Israel and the CIA denied involvement, and an unconfirmed April 2024 Iran International report says he lives in the US under CIA witness protection. Both are described as spies/defectors, not whistleblowers.

## Key Question 6: Jeffrey Sterling (United States) and Operation Merlin

### Takeaway
Jeffrey Sterling, a CIA case officer (hired 1993) who ran the Russian asset "Merlin" in the plan to pass flawed nuclear-weapon designs to Iran, filed a racial-discrimination complaint (2000), sued (2001), was fired (effective 31 Jan 2002), saw his suit dismissed on state-secrets grounds (2004, affirmed 2005), and took his concerns about Merlin to Senate Intelligence Committee staff in March 2003; after James Risen's State of War (2006) described the operation, Sterling was indicted on 22 Dec 2010, convicted on nine counts on 26 Jan 2015 on circumstantial evidence, sentenced by Judge Brinkema to 42 months on 11 May 2015 (guidelines 235 months), lost most of his appeal in June 2017, was released January 2018 and published Unwanted Spy: The Persecution of an American Whistleblower (2019). DOJ framed him as a vindictive leaker; advocacy groups framed him as a whistleblower prosecuted under the Espionage Act while David Petraeus got probation.

### Cited Findings
- Career: "He joined as a CIA case officer in 1993 and held a top secret clearance"; "Sterling was officially terminated from the CIA on January 31, 2002, but he had been 'outprocessed' and effectively removed from service in October 2001" — [Wikipedia, "Jeffrey Alexander Sterling"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Alexander_Sterling)
- Discrimination complaint: "in August 2000... Sterling, who is African-American, filed a racial-discrimination complaint against the CIA"; the EEO office found it "without merit"; he sued in August 2001; "The lawsuit was dismissed in March 2004, following the government's invocation of the state secrets doctrine"; the Fourth Circuit affirmed in Sterling v. Tenet, 416 F.3d 338 (4th Cir. 2005), reasoning he "could not prove discrimination without exposing some classified details of his covert employment" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Alexander_Sterling); [FindLaw, Sterling v. Tenet](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-4th-circuit/1639333.html)
- Senate complaint: "Sterling approached the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) with information about Operation Merlin in March 2003. He told the staffers that the program had not only been a failure, but may have assisted the Iranians in advancing their nuclear program" (secondary account) — [Emptywheel](https://www.emptywheel.net/2015/02/21/what-was-the-cia-really-doing-with-merlin-by-2003/); supporters note that "in March 2003, he took his concerns about Operation Merlin to the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence" — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/cia-officer-jeffrey-sterling-sentenced-prison-latest-blow-governments-war-journalism/)
- Operation Merlin: Risen's 2006 book described "a plan in which a CIA agent was to deliver flawed nuclear blueprints to Iran in the hopes that they would spend years trying to develop a product that would never work"; "The CIA has rejected that characterization" — [JURIST, 2017](https://www.jurist.org/news/2017/06/federal-court-of-appeals-upholds-almost-all-convictions-of-former-cia-officer); prosecutors described Merlin as using "a former Russian scientist to market and sell Iran schematics for a flawed fire set" — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/?p=246365)
- Risen subpoena: "the prosecution subpoenaed Risen to testify and reveal his journalistic sources, an effort which Risen and his attorneys contested"; Risen was subpoenaed in April 2010 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Alexander_Sterling); Fourth Circuit opinion on the Risen subpoena (2013) — [RCFP](https://www.rcfp.org/wp-content/uploads/imported/20130719_134812_risen_opinion.pdf)
- Indictment: a grand jury in the Eastern District of Virginia "returned a 10-count indictment on Dec. 22, 2010, which was unsealed when he was arrested," charging "six counts of unauthorized disclosure of national defense information, plus one count each of unlawful retention of national defense information, mail fraud, unauthorized conveyance of government property and obstruction of justice"; he pleaded not guilty in January 2011 — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/40951509); [CSM, 6 Jan 2011](https://proof.csmonitor.com/USA/Justice/2011/0106/Former-covert-CIA-agent-charged-with-leaking-secrets-to-newspaper)
- Conviction: "Sterling was instead convicted by a jury in January on a case built of mostly circumstantial evidence"; the judge called it "very powerful circumstantial evidence"; convicted 26 January 2015 — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/cia-officer-jeffrey-sterling-sentenced-prison-latest-blow-governments-war-journalism/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Alexander_Sterling); the jury convicted on nine counts — [Sterling Family Fund page](https://www.gofundme.com/lg8xxs); [JURIST, May 2015](https://www.jurist.org/news/2015/05/former-cia-officer-sentenced-to-42-months-in-prison-for-iran-leaks)
- Sentence (11 May 2015): "Coming in 80 percent lower than guidelines recommended, a federal judge sentenced ex-CIA agent Jeffrey Sterling to just 42 months in prison"; "Two hundred and thirty-five months represented the base recommendation"; Brinkema: "The guidelines are too high"; she "viewed exposing clues regarding the identity of a CIA asset the government had code-named 'Merlin' as the gravest part of his conduct" and said protecting a person who serves the US government "is a more critical secret than the operation itself" — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/?p=246365); [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/former-cia-officer-sentenced-42-months-prison-leaking-classified-information-and-obstruction)
- DOJ framing: US Attorney Dana Boente said Sterling acted "for his own vindictive purposes"; "The government's position was that Sterling was motivated to leak the information as a result of his failed suit against the CIA for discrimination" — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/cia-officer-jeffrey-sterling-sentenced-prison-latest-blow-governments-war-journalism/); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/world/jeffrey-sterling-ex-cia-gets-prison-term-for-leaking-info-to-reporter-1.3070425)
- Petraeus comparison: Sterling's attorneys argued "David Petraeus received a plea deal and great leniency despite comparable charges" and pointed to John Kiriakou's 2.5-year sentence; Desmond Tutu and Holly Sterling argued selective prosecution — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ex-cia-officer-convicted-of-iran-leak-seeks-lenient-sentence-citing-ex-director-petraeus-case.amp); [Transcend/Common Dreams](https://www.transcend.org/tms/?p=58324)

- Critics of the evidence: "the government presented evidence that Sterling and Risen spoke for four minutes and 11 seconds in advance of the first story" — [Emptywheel, 22 June 2017](https://www.emptywheel.net/2017/06/22/in-opinion-mostly-rejecting-jeffrey-sterling-appeal-fourth-circuit-criminalizes-unclassified-tips/); a Truthout analysis titled "Merlin Meets Curveball" — [Truthout](https://truthout.org/articles/the-sterling-trial-merlin-meets-curveball/)
- Appeal: "Argued: December 6, 2016 Decided: June 22, 2017"; "The three-judge panel affirmed all but one of Jeffrey Sterling's convictions and said there was no need for him to be resentenced," reversing one count "on the grounds of improper venue"; Judge Diaz wrote the majority, Traxler dissented in part; the panel found the jury rationally concluded "that he passed along at least some classified information about the CIA's efforts to thwart Iran's nuclear weapons program"; "Sterling maintains he was not a source" — [Fourth Circuit opinion (FAS)](https://sgp.fas.org/jud/sterling/062217-appeal.pdf); [JURIST, 2017](https://www.jurist.org/news/2017/06/federal-court-of-appeals-upholds-almost-all-convictions-of-former-cia-officer)
- Release and memoir: "released from prison in January 2018"; Unwanted Spy: The Persecution of an American Whistleblower, New York: Bold Type Books, 2019 — [Des Moines Public Library catalog](https://catalog.dmpl.org/Record/102945); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/43886145)
- Clemency: about eight months into the term, Holly Sterling petitioned the White House for a pardon — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/cia-officer-jeffrey-sterling-sentenced-prison-latest-blow-governments-war-journalism/)

### Inferences
- Sterling is the paradigmatic contested whistleblower: he used a lawful internal channel (SSCI) in 2003, which supporters cite as proof of whistleblower intent, yet he was convicted for an alleged unauthorized disclosure to a journalist that he denies making; the court never found that he intended to expose wrongdoing, only that he disclosed national defense information.
- Brinkema's sentencing emphasis on the asset's identity, not the operation, suggests the court treated the harm as counterintelligence exposure rather than policy embarrassment, while the 80% downward variance implicitly acknowledged the Petraeus disparity argument.

### Gaps
- Whether the tenth indictment count was dismissed before trial or merged (sources say 10 counts indicted, 9 convicted) was not clarified.
- Risen's own chapter text and the CIA's formal position on Merlin's effects were not retrieved.
- Senate Intelligence Committee records of the 2003 contact are not public; the account rests on trial testimony as summarized by bloggers and advocacy groups.

## Key Question 7: Edalat-e Ali ("Ali's Justice") and other hack-based exposures (brief)

### Takeaway
Beginning 22 August 2021 the hacktivist group Edalat-e Ali released at least 16 CCTV videos from Evin prison showing guards beating prisoners, overcrowding and self-harm, plus a control-room takeover taunting President Raisi; Iran's prisons chief admitted the footage was genuine and apologized to "God, our dear supreme leader, the great nation and the honorable prison guards," and the judiciary chief ordered an inquiry. The group (which says it operates inside Iran) later hit Telewebion (2022), state TV (2023) and leaked 3 million judiciary documents (2024), while "Black Reward" leaked Atomic Energy Organization emails (2022); these are external intrusions, not insider whistleblowing, though officials have blamed "a specific foreign country."

### Cited Findings
- "The group began releasing the footage on August 22 [2021]. By late August, 16 videos had been released, mostly from 2020 and 2021, though some dated back to 2015 and 2016"; the hackers "claimed to have recorded hours of footage" — [Center for Human Rights in Iran](https://iranhumanrights.org/2021/08/hacked-iranian-prison-footage-provides-limited-glimpse-of-right-of-abuses/)
- Amnesty's count: "seven videos showing prison guards beating or otherwise ill-treating prisoners, three showing overcrowded prison rooms, three showing assaults by inmates on other inmates, two showing self-harm, and one depicting a solitary confinement cell"; Amnesty said the content included clear examples of torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading punishment — [CHRI](https://iranhumanrights.org/2021/08/hacked-iranian-prison-footage-provides-limited-glimpse-of-right-of-abuses/); [MENA Prison Forum](https://www.menaprisonforum.org/blog_detail/16/)
- Control room: "One clip shows a guard jumping up as the monitors in front of him flash red and then go black one by one"; screens carried a taunt aimed at Raisi describing the prison as a stain on his image — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/mena/iran/2021/08/24/iran-prison-head-sorry-as-leaked-videos-show-beatings-and-abuse-in-evin-jail/); [Al Jazeera, 24 Aug 2021](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2021/8/24/iran-confirms-leaked-footage-of-harsh-conditions-in-evin-prison)
- Official response: prisons chief Mohammad Mehdi Haj Mohammadi "accepted responsibility for the unacceptable behaviour and pledged to try to prevent any repeat"; his message apologized to "God, our dear supreme leader, the great nation and the honorable prison guards"; judiciary chief Mohseni-Ejei "ordered an investigation into the treatment of prisoners at Evin" — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2021/8/24/iran-confirms-leaked-footage-of-harsh-conditions-in-evin-prison); [RFE/RL, "Hacked Videos Force The Unthinkable"](https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-evin-prison-abuse/31429924.html); [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/?p=120656); Tehran Bureau noted he apologized to the leader and guards "Rather than apologize to the inmates" — [Tehran Bureau](https://tehranbureau.com/?p=6301)
- Former inmates said the footage "provides a severely limited glimpse" and does not capture "white torture, psychological and mental abuse of detainees" in solitary and interrogation rooms — [CHRI](https://iranhumanrights.org/2021/08/hacked-iranian-prison-footage-provides-limited-glimpse-of-right-of-abuses/)
- The group's self-description, in written answers to Radio Farda: "Our goal is to expose the regime's inhumane activities against the Iranian people and not allow the regime to hide... We want the release of political prisoners"; it denied operating from abroad and credited "compatriots" inside Iran — [RFE/RL exclusive](https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-hacktivists-prison-abuses/31564796.html)
- Later operations: Telewebion breach (Feb 2022); interruption of a presidential speech on state TV (Feb 2023) airing "Death to Khamenei"; in February 2024 release of "more than 3 million documents from the court case management system of Branch 26 of Tehran's Islamic Revolutionary Court," listing 44 journalists targeted and National Security Council deliberations after Mahsa Amini's death — [RFE/RL, 2024](https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-judiciary-hack-targeting-journalists/32831893.html); [Iran International, Feb 2024](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202402201687); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/142172/hacktivism/iranian-state-tv-hacked.html)
- Black Reward (Oct-Nov 2022): claimed data from "324 mailboxes, containing more than 100,000 messages and 50 GB" of an Atomic Energy Organization subsidiary, demanding release of protesters; the AEOI acknowledged the breach but blamed "unauthorized access from a specific foreign country"; the group also claimed to wipe Fars News Agency data — [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/iran-nuclear-emails-hack-leak-black-reward/); [Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202210225387); [Hackread](https://www.hackread.com/fars-news-agency-website-iran-hacked/)

### Inferences
- The Evin leak achieved what no insider whistleblower has in Iran, an official admission and apology, precisely because the evidence was video obtained externally and could not be pinned on a prosecutable individual; this underscores why exposure of the Iranian security state increasingly comes via hacks rather than insiders.
- Officials' attribution to a "foreign country" and the groups' anonymity make these events categorically different from whistleblowing: no identified insider, no internal channel, and no personal accountability.

### Gaps
- The identity, size and location of Edalat-e Ali remain unverified; claims of operating inside Iran are self-reported.
- Reports of a 2024 Ghezel Hesar prison leak and the "Tapandegan" group were not found in retrievable sources.

## Key Question 8: Does Iran protect whistleblowers, and what happens to insiders who expose the security services?

### Takeaway
Iran has no dedicated whistleblower-protection statute: the 2011 Law on Promotion of Administrative Integrity and Combating Corruption contains limited protective clauses (Article 17) and a 2019 Majlis bill "Support for Whistleblowers of Economic and Administrative Corruption" was signed by 94 MPs, but its passage is unconfirmed and Iranian scholars say protection measures are "neglected." In practice, corruption whistleblowers (MP Ahmad Alirezabeigi, Vahid Ashtari, Yashar Soltani) are jailed for "spreading lies," and insiders touching security or nuclear matters face "espionage" and "corruption on earth" charges carrying death (Amiri 2016; Reza Asgari and Jalal Hajizavar 2020), with a June 2025 law widening capital espionage to any "operational" cooperation with Israel or the US and a surge of spy executions in 2025-2026.

### Cited Findings
- 2011 law: Iranian scholarship records "the enactment of the Administrative Health Promotion and Anti-Corruption Law in 2011" — [Yazd IAU journal](https://imr.yazd.iau.ir/article_710045.html?lang=en); a Persian summary of Article 17 describes transfers "at the person's request to another suitable location, without any reduction in salary, benefits or acquired rights" and state compensation "where immediate compensation from the perpetrator is not possible" — [Edalatsara, Article 17](https://edalatsara.com/rules/%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AA-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1%DB%8C-%D9%88-%D9%85%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%84%D9%87-%D8%A8%D8%A7-%D9%81%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%AF/%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%87-17)
- 2019 bill: "94 lawmakers signed a bill titled 'Support for Whistleblowers of Economic and Administrative Corruption'" — [Al-Monitor, Feb 2019](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2019/02/iran-corruption-whistleblowers-bill-lawmakers-mps-rouhani.html); an Iranian academic article says Iran's "first step in this field was offering financial support to whistleblowers, but its action is only based on the support aspect" and "protection measures are neglected"; a Persian study found the parliamentary plan "in most cases has not followed the principles" of Transparency International — [Yazd IAU journal](https://imr.yazd.iau.ir/article_710045.html?lang=en); [Ensani.ir](https://www.ensani.ir/fa/article/555408/%D9%88%D8%A7%DA%A9%D8%A7%D9%88%DB%8C-%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%DA%A9%D8%B1%D8%AF-%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B4%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%81%DB%8C%D8%AA-%D8%A8%DB%8C%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%84%D9%84-%D9%88-%D8%B7%D8%B1%D8%AD-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%B4%D8%A7%DA%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%81%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%AF)
- UNCAC Article 33 (Iran is a party and active in the review process) only requires that states "consider incorporating... appropriate measures" to protect good-faith reporters — [Transparency International](https://transparency.org/en/publications/whistleblower-protection-and-the-un-convention-against-corruption)
- Corruption whistleblowers jailed: MP Ahmad Alirezabeigi, who alleged bribery by a former minister, "was sentenced to one year behind bars on charges of 'spreading lies'" and banned from speaking in parliament (2023) — [Iran International, July 2023](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202307187073); Vahid Ashtari, who exposed the Ghalibaf family's Turkey shopping trip, was "sentenced to two years in prison for 'publishing false information intended to cause public harm'" (2022) — [Iran International, July 2022](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202207248670); journalist Yashar Soltani was jailed for 13 months for "spreading lies to disturb the public mind" after earlier receiving five years over Tehran land-deal reporting — [AP via ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/International/wireStory/iran-imprisons-prominent-whistleblower-110965713); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/world/iran-whistleblower-convicted-over-false-information-1132731.html)
- Security insiders: Amiri was "charged with espionage for enemies of the Islamic Republic" and hanged (2016) — [ISNA via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/iran/2016/iran-160807-isna01.htm); nuclear negotiator Abdolrasoul Dorri Esfahani got five years even though the intelligence minister said he "was collaborating with the counterespionage department of his ministry" — [Radio Farda](https://en.radiofarda.com/a/iran-nuclear-spy-accusations/28790466.html); in July 2020 Iran executed former defense-ministry employee Reza Asgari for spying for the CIA and Jalal Hajizavar after a confession of taking CIA payment — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/iran-executes-alleged-spy-reza-asgari-for-selling-us-cia-info-on-iranian-missiles); [VOA via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/library/news/2020/intell-200714-voa01.htm)
- Historical charge patterns: Hashemi, "corruption on earth" (1987) — [AP via ECU](https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/encore/ncgre000/00000097/00096733/00096733.pdf); Ganji, "collecting confidential information harmful to national security and spreading propaganda against the Islamic system" (2001) — [Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mde130222001en.pdf)
- 2025 espionage law: passed by parliament in late June 2025 after the 12-day war; formally an intensification of punishment for "espionage and cooperation with the Zionist regime and hostile states"; it "classifies any collaboration with Israel and the United States as 'corruption on earth', which is punishable by death"; the Guardian Council first returned it in July citing ambiguities, then approved the revised text, which punishes "any operational action" against security including intelligence work for Israel or the US with "execution and property confiscation" and extends death eligibility to "any cyber warfare" and to sharing images with foreign media or "publishing false news" deemed a security threat; Starlink possession is punishable by imprisonment and by death if done "with intent to act against the system or for espionage" — [RFE/RL via GlobalSecurity, 4 July 2025](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/iran/2025/iran-250704-rferl01.htm); [Rudaw, 14 July 2025](https://www.rudaw.net/english/middleeast/iran/14072025); [Iran International, Oct 2025](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202510093488); [Iran International, June 2025](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202506293099)
- Critics: the bill's "deliberately vague" terms such as "collaboration" or "spreading false news" give "judicial and security bodies broad discretion to define opposition activity as treason or intelligence work" — [Women Journalists Without Chains](https://wjwc.org/reports/irans-espionage-law-a-dissent-silencing-tool)
- Executions: "By late September 2025... nine people had been put to death for espionage since the June war"; rights groups say Iran executed at least 1,639 people in 2025, a record since 1989; in early August 2026 the judiciary's Mizan said two men were hanged for transmitting coordinates of sensitive sites to Mossad; Amnesty's MENA deputy director said "The authorities are weaponizing the death penalty to instill fear and assert control" and that capital punishment for espionage without intentional killing violates international law — [Asharq Al-Awsat, Aug 2026](https://english.aawsat.com/node/5302825); [Newsweek](https://newsweek.com/iran-executes-spy-israel-2130986); [Iran International, June 2025](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202506210488); [Madhyamam (Amnesty on four Kurds)](https://english.madhyamam.com/middle-east/iran-executes-four-kurds-accused-of-spying-for-israel-1251932)

### Inferences
- The pattern across 1987-2026 is consistent: disclosures about corruption are prosecuted as "spreading lies"/"propaganda against the state," and disclosures touching the security or nuclear apparatus are prosecuted as "espionage" or "corruption on earth," with no legal defense of public interest; the 2025 law formalizes death as the default for security-related cooperation with the US or Israel.
- Protective law in Iran is confined to administrative corruption reporting and even there is described by Iranian scholars as incomplete; nothing extends to intelligence or military insiders, which explains why exposure of the security services has come from defectors abroad (Mesbahi, Asgari), ex-insiders turned journalists (Ganji), factional leaks (1986, 2001) or hackers (2021-2024) rather than protected whistleblowers.
