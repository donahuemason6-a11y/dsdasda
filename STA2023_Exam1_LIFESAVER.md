# SIDE 1

## SYMBOL DECODER (read this first)
- **Σ** ("sigma," big) = "add them all up." Σx = add all the values.
- **x** = one data value. **N** = how many values in the POPULATION. **n** = how many in the SAMPLE.
- **μ** ("mu") = population mean (average). **x̄** ("x-bar") = sample mean. Same math, different letter.
- **σ** ("sigma," small) = population standard deviation. **σ²** = population variance (= σ squared).
- **s** = sample standard deviation. **s²** = sample variance.
- **p** = population proportion; **p̂** ("p-hat") = sample proportion (a percent written as a decimal).
- **M** = median (middle value). **Q₁ / Q₂ / Q₃** = quartiles (25% / 50% / 75% marks; Q₂ = median).
- **IQR** = Q₃ − Q₁. **LF / UF** = lower / upper fence (outlier cutoffs). **z** = z-score (how many SDs from the mean).
- **RF** = relative frequency = part ÷ total. **°** = degrees (pie chart). **√** = square root. **( )²** = square it.
- **Greek letters + capital N = POPULATION (everyone). Regular letters + small n = SAMPLE (some of them).**

## CHAPTER 1 — COLLECTING DATA

### If asked: "identify the population / sample / individuals / parameter / statistic" ★Q1
- **Population** = the WHOLE group you want to know about. Look at what the study is about: "all people who could follow the diet," "all US adults with high blood pressure."
- **Sample** = the smaller group actually measured. Look for the number: "80 participants," "165 adults."
- **Individual** = ONE member (one participant, one student).
- **Parameter** = a number describing the whole population. Usually UNKNOWN → describe it in words: "the true average cholesterol drop for everyone on the diet (μ)."
- **Statistic** = a number computed from the sample = the number they GIVE you: "average drop of 15 points (x̄ = 15)."
- Memory: **P**arameter ↔ **P**opulation; **S**tatistic ↔ **S**ample. If a number is stated for ALL (e.g., 0.849 of all students), that number IS the parameter.
- Statistics process: (1) state the question, (2) collect data, (3) describe it (graphs, averages), (4) inference (use the sample to say something about the population).

### If asked: "qualitative or quantitative / discrete or continuous / level of measurement" ★Q2 — answer ALL THREE
**Step 1 — Qualitative or Quantitative?**
- **Qualitative (categorical)** = words, labels, categories: car type, phone brand, yes/no, "strongly agree," eye color. ALSO **rankings (1st, 2nd, 3rd)** and number-labels (ZIP code, ID) — they look like numbers but are labels.
- **Quantitative** = actual numbers you can average: temperature, number of pets, income, hours of sleep, height.

**Step 2 — Discrete or Continuous?** (only for quantitative; if qualitative write **"Not applicable (not quantitative)"**)
- **Discrete** = you COUNT it; whole numbers with gaps ("number of…", "how many…"). Can't have 2.5 pets.
- **Continuous** = you MEASURE it; any value in a range, decimals allowed: temperature, time/hours, weight, height, distance, income ($), age, grade (%).

**Step 3 — Level of measurement: ask these questions IN ORDER**
1. Can the values be put in a meaningful order? **NO → Nominal** (car type, phone brand, yes/no, ZIP, gender).
2. Yes, order. Are the gaps between values equal/meaningful? **NO → Ordinal** (rankings, small/medium/large, strongly agree…disagree, letter grade, education level, young/middle/old, star rating).
3. Yes, gaps meaningful. Does 0 mean "none at all"? **NO → Interval** (**temperature °F/°C** — 0° is not "no temperature," 80° is not twice 40°; calendar year, clock time, SAT score).
4. Yes, 0 = none. **→ Ratio** (any count, money, time, weight, height, age, distance, grams, % grade).

Shortcuts: qualitative → always Nominal or Ordinal. Quantitative → always Interval or Ratio, and it is **Ratio unless it's temperature/year/clock time (Interval)**.

Worked (★Q2): (a) Transmission type → Qualitative; Not applicable; Nominal. (b) Number of pets → Quantitative; Discrete; Ratio. (c) Temperature in °F → Quantitative; Continuous; Interval. (d) Ranking of top 5 players → Qualitative; Not applicable; Ordinal.
More: education level → Qual, N/A, Ordinal. Grams of whole grains per day → Quant, Continuous, Ratio. Number of vending machines / students → Quant, Discrete, Ratio. Prepared for class (yes/no) → Qual, N/A, Nominal. Age in years / income in $ / hours of sleep / algebra grade % → Quant, Continuous, Ratio. Internet provider / phone type → Qual, N/A, Nominal. "Working with numbers upsets me" (agree…disagree) → Qual, N/A, Ordinal.

### If asked: "observational study or experiment? explanatory / response variable? can we claim causation?" ★Q3
- **Explanatory variable** = the thing that might cause a change (the input/cause): screen time, the drug, type of music lesson.
- **Response variable** = the outcome measured (the result): GPA, blood pressure, IQ score.
- **Observational study** = researchers only WATCH, ASK, SURVEY, RECORD, TRACK, or compare groups that already exist. Nobody is told what to do.
- **Designed experiment** = the researcher ASSIGNS the treatment: "randomly assigned to groups," "given the drug / a placebo," "control group."
- **CAUSATION RULE: observational → only association, answer "No" to causation. Experiment → can claim causation.** Why: a **lurking variable** (something not measured, like sleep or study habits) could be the real cause.
- Worked (★Q3, survey of screen time and GPA): (a) Observational (only surveyed). (b) Explanatory = daily screen time. (c) Response = GPA. (d) Causation? **No**. Notes: kids randomly assigned to xylophone/singing/listening → experiment; children given milk with/without xylitol → experiment; surveys of new mothers / faculty / teens tracked for years → observational.
- **Census** = measure the ENTIRE population. **Simple random sample (SRS)** = every possible group of n has the same chance. To take one: (1) list everyone (the "frame") and number them 1 to N; (2) generate n random numbers; those people are the sample (435 House members → number 1–435, generate 5 random numbers).

### If asked: "what type of bias?" ★Q4 — ask three questions in order
1. **Who got asked?** If the wrong or only part of the group was asked → **SAMPLING bias**. ("Survey sent only to headquarters," "polled only its own readers," "people at the mall," "online poll people volunteer for.")
2. **Did the chosen people answer?** If many didn't and they differ from those who did → **NONRESPONSE bias**. ("Only 20% responded, mostly the unhappy ones," "refused," "hung up.")
3. **Are the answers true?** If people lie, give "safe" answers, or the question pushes an answer → **RESPONSE bias**. ("Answers management wants to hear," leading/loaded question, embarrassing topic, mistyped data.)
- **Nonsampling error** = mistakes from those biases (possible even in a census). **Sampling error** = the natural difference between a sample's number and the population's number just because you didn't measure everyone. Literary Digest 1936: sampling bias (only readers, car and phone owners) + nonresponse (only 2.38 million of 10 million replied) → predicted the wrong winner.

## CHAPTER 2 — TABLES AND GRAPHS

### If given a table of categories with counts: "fill in relative frequency / degrees / percent" ★Q5
- **Frequency** = the count. **Relative frequency = count ÷ total** (decimal; all RFs add to 1). **Degrees = RF × 360** (all add to 360). **Percent = RF × 100.**
- Backwards: **count = RF × total**; missing RF = 1 − (the others); missing count = total − (the others).
- Worked (★Q5, total 60+50+40+30+20 = 200): Sports 60/200 = **0.3000**, 0.3000 × 360 = **108°**. Movies 50/200 = **0.2500**, 90°. Shopping 0.2000 × 360 = **72°**. Reading 30/200 = 0.1500, 54°. Other 0.1000, 36°. Check: 108+90+72+54+36 = 360 ✓. (b) Most popular = biggest count → Sports. (c) Reading = 0.15 → **15%**. (d) Sports or Movies = (60+50)/200 = 110/200 = 0.55 = 55% > 50% → **True**.
- **Bar graph:** one bar per category, height = count; gaps between bars; **axis must start at 0, equal widths, 2-D**. **Pareto** = bars tallest→shortest. **Pie chart:** slice angle = RF × 360°; use for parts of a whole; use bars to compare specific categories. Comparing two groups of different sizes → compare **relative** frequencies, never raw counts.

### If given classes like 40–49, 50–59 …: "class limits / class width / how many / what percent" ★Q6
- **Lower class limit** = first number of the class (60 in 60–69). **Upper class limit** = last number (69).
- **Class width = start of next class − start of this class: 50 − 40 = 10.** (NOT 49 − 40 = 9 — classic trap.)
- **How many in a range** → add the counts of those classes. **What percent** → (those counts ÷ total) × 100. **Most frequent class** → the biggest count; answer with the interval (70–79).
- **Histogram** = bar graph of these classes, bars touching. **Open-ended** class = "< 75" or "300 or more."
- Worked (★Q6, 40 students; 40–49: 4, 50–59: 6, 60–69: 10, 70–79: 12, 80–89: 8): (a) 3rd class 60–69 → lower 60, upper 69. (b) width 50 − 40 = **10**. (c) 70 or higher: 12 + 8 = 20 → 20/40 = **50%**. (d) below 60: 4 + 6 = **10**. (e) biggest count 12 → **70–79**.

### If asked: "what shape?" ★Q7 — look where the long thin TAIL points
- **Bell-shaped**: hump in the middle, mirror image. Heights, weights, IQ, most test scores.
- **Uniform**: flat, all values equally common. Fair die rolls, random digits.
- **Skewed RIGHT**: tail to the right = a few unusually BIG values. Salaries (a few executives earn millions), house prices, waiting times (most short, a few very long).
- **Skewed LEFT**: tail to the left = a few unusually SMALL values. Easy exam where most score high and a few bomb.
- Worked (★Q7): salaries → skewed right; heights of 10-year-olds → bell-shaped; die rolled 200 times → uniform; waiting times → skewed right.
- Link to Ch 3: skewed right → mean > median; skewed left → mean < median; symmetric → mean ≈ median.

### Other graphs
- **Stem-and-leaf:** stem = all digits but the last, leaf = last digit (56 → 5 | 6). List every stem in order (even empty ones), leaves in order, add a key. Scores 56, 60, 66, 68, 80, 82, 82, 84, 88, 90, 92, 94, 98, 98 → 5|6 · 6|0 6 8 · 7| · 8|0 2 2 4 8 · 9|0 2 4 8 8.
- **Time-series plot:** time on the bottom, value on the side, dots connected. "Highest year" = highest dot.
- **Misleading graph:** vertical axis doesn't start at 0 (small differences look huge), bars of different widths, 3-D. Answer = name the flaw AND the fix ("start the axis at 0").

# SIDE 2

## CHAPTER 3 — DESCRIBING DATA WITH NUMBERS
Running example = ★Q8–Q11: commute times of ALL 10 employees (so it's a **population**), already sorted: **18, 22, 25, 28, 30, 31, 33, 35, 36, 40** (N = 10).

### If asked: "find the mean" ★Q8a, Q9a
Add all values, divide by how many. Population: **μ = Σx ÷ N**. Sample: **x̄ = Σx ÷ n**. (Same math; use μ if the data is everyone / says "population," x̄ if it says "sample.")
Worked: 18+22+25+28+30+31+33+35+36+40 = 298 → **μ = 298/10 = 29.8 min**. Sample of first 5 (18, 22, 25, 28, 30): 123/5 → **x̄ = 24.6 min**.

### If asked: "find the median" ★Q8c
(1) SORT the data. (2) Count n. (3) n odd → the middle value (position (n+1)/2). n even → average the two middle values (positions n/2 and n/2 + 1).
Worked: n = 10 (even) → 5th and 6th values are 30 and 31 → **M = (30+31)/2 = 30.5 min**. Odd example: 76, 80, 84, **86**, 90, 92, 94 → M = 86.

### If asked: "find the mode" ★Q8d
The value that appears MOST. Two tie → both are modes ("bimodal": 7, 12, 12, 20, 20, 25 → 12 and 20). Nothing repeats → write **"no mode"**. Worked: every commute time appears once → **no mode**.

### If asked: "based on mean vs median, describe the shape" ★Q8e
The mean gets pulled toward the tail; the median doesn't. **mean > median → skewed right. mean < median → skewed left. mean ≈ median → symmetric.**
Worked: mean 29.8 < median 30.5 → **skewed left (slightly)**. Write the comparison and the rule.
**Resistant** = not thrown off by extreme values. Median and IQR are resistant; mean, range, SD are not. (Add a 70-min commuter to 5, 18, 23, 23, 26, 36, 43: mean jumps 24.9 → 30.5, median only 23 → 24.5.) Symmetric data → report mean & SD; skewed data → report median & IQR.

### If asked: "find the range"
Biggest − smallest: 40 − 18 = **22 min**. (Easy, but one extreme value wrecks it.)

### If asked: "find the variance and standard deviation" ★Q8b (population), Q9b (sample)
**What it means:** standard deviation (SD) ≈ how far the values typically are from the mean. Small SD = bunched up; big SD = spread out. Variance = SD squared (a middle step).
**Why the recipe:** (value − mean) adds up to 0, so we SQUARE each difference (all positive), average the squares (= variance), then √ to undo the squaring (= SD).
**THE RULE: "population" / all the data → divide by N. "sample" → divide by n − 1.** The word *sample* is your trigger.
**Steps (draw this table):** (1) find the mean. (2) column: value − mean. (3) column: square it. (4) add the squares. (5) divide by N (population) or n − 1 (sample) → **variance**. (6) √ → **standard deviation**. Check: the (value − mean) column should add to 0.

Worked ★Q8b (population, μ = 29.8):

| x | x − μ | (x − μ)² |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | −11.8 | 139.24 |
| 22 | −7.8 | 60.84 |
| 25 | −4.8 | 23.04 |
| 28 | −1.8 | 3.24 |
| 30 | 0.2 | 0.04 |
| 31 | 1.2 | 1.44 |
| 33 | 3.2 | 10.24 |
| 35 | 5.2 | 27.04 |
| 36 | 6.2 | 38.44 |
| 40 | 10.2 | 104.04 |
| add | 0 ✓ | **407.60** |

**σ² = 407.60 ÷ 10 = 40.76 min²**. **σ = √40.76 = 6.38 min** (6.3844 unrounded). (If the same 10 were called a sample: 407.60 ÷ 9 = 45.29; s = 6.73.)

Worked ★Q9b (sample 18, 22, 25, 28, 30; x̄ = 24.6): differences −6.6, −2.6, 0.4, 3.4, 5.4 → squares 43.56, 6.76, 0.16, 11.56, 29.16 → add = **91.20** → **s² = 91.20 ÷ (5 − 1) = 91.20 ÷ 4 = 22.8 min²** (÷4, NOT ÷5). If s is asked: √22.8 = **4.77 min**.
**Calculator:** enter the data in 1-Var Stats. It shows TWO SDs: **σx = population (÷N)** and **Sx = sample (÷(n−1))** — pick the right one. Variance = square the UNROUNDED SD (6.3844² = 40.76; never 6.4² = 40.96). Still write the table — steps are required. TI-30XS/36X: [data] → enter in L1 → [2nd][data] → 1-Var Stats → CALC. Casio fx-991EX: MENU → Statistics → 1-Variable → enter → [OPTN] → 1-Variable Calc. Casio fx-300ES: MODE → STAT → 1-VAR → enter → AC → SHIFT 1 → Var. TI-84: STAT → Edit → L1; STAT → CALC → 1-Var Stats.

### If asked: "Empirical Rule / 68–95–99.7 / between what two values / what percent between" ★Q11 (bell-shaped data only)
- About **68%** of values are within 1 SD of the mean (μ − σ to μ + σ); **95%** within 2 SDs; **99.7%** within 3 SDs.
- Pieces: mean to 1 SD = **34%** (each side); 1 SD to 2 SD = **13.5%**; 2 SD to 3 SD = **2.35%**; beyond 3 SD = **0.15%**; above the mean = 50%.
- **Steps:** (1) write μ and σ (round σ to 1 decimal if the question's numbers only match a rounded σ, and say so). (2) Compute the six landmarks: μ ± 1σ, μ ± 2σ, μ ± 3σ. (3) Find the question's numbers among the landmarks. (4) Add up the pieces between them.

Worked ★Q11 (μ = 29.8; his numbers 23.4 and 36.2 = 29.8 ∓ 6.4, so use **σ ≈ 6.4**): 1σ → 23.4 and 36.2; 2σ (12.8) → 17.0 and 42.6; 3σ (19.2) → 10.6 and 49.0.

```
10.6    17.0    23.4    29.8    36.2    42.6    49.0
 |2.35%  |13.5%  | 34%   | 34%   |13.5%  |2.35%  |
```

(a) 95% ↔ 2 SDs: 29.8 − 12.8 = 17.0 and 29.8 + 12.8 = 42.6 → **between 17.0 and 42.6 min**. (b) 23.4 to 36.2 = 1 SD each side → **68%**. (c) 36.2 to 42.6 = from 1 SD to 2 SD above → (95 − 68) ÷ 2 = **13.5%**.
Quick answers: above μ+1σ = 16%; above μ+2σ = 2.5%; μ+1σ to μ+3σ = 13.5 + 2.35 = 15.85%; μ−1σ to μ+2σ = 34 + 34 + 13.5 = 81.5%. Notes Ex: waist μ = 48.5, σ = 4.8 → 95% between 38.9 and 58.1; 34.1 to 62.9 = ±3σ → 99.7%; 53.3 to 62.9 = +1σ to +3σ → 15.85%. (Chebyshev: not tested.)

### If asked: "z-score" or "who did relatively better?"
**z = (value − mean) ÷ SD** = how many SDs from the mean; positive = above, negative = below, bigger z = relatively higher. ACT 27 (mean 18, SD 6): z = (27 − 18)/6 = **1.50**. SAT 1200 (mean 1000, SD 200): z = 200/200 = **1.00** → the ACT student did relatively better. Commute 40 min: z = (40 − 29.8)/6.38 = **1.60**.
**Percentile:** "95th percentile" = 95% of the data is at or below you. Median = 50th percentile.

### If asked: "quartiles / five-number summary" ★Q10a
(1) SORT. (2) **Q₂ = median** of everything. (3) **Q₁ = median of the LOWER half; Q₃ = median of the UPPER half.** If n is odd, leave the overall median OUT of both halves. (4) **Five-number summary = Min, Q₁, M, Q₃, Max.**
Worked (n = 10 → halves of 5): lower 18, 22, **25**, 28, 30 → Q₁ = 25; upper 31, 33, **35**, 36, 40 → Q₃ = 35; M = 30.5. **Summary: 18, 25, 30.5, 35, 40.**
Odd example: 76, 80, 84 | 86 | 90, 92, 94 → Q₁ = 80, Q₂ = 86, Q₃ = 92. Even example with a low value: 32, 76, 80, 84 | 86, 90, 92, 94 → Q₁ = (76+80)/2 = 78, Q₂ = 85, Q₃ = (90+92)/2 = 91.

### If asked: "IQR / fences / outliers" ★Q10b,c
(1) **IQR = Q₃ − Q₁.** (2) **1.5 × IQR.** (3) **Lower fence = Q₁ − 1.5·IQR; Upper fence = Q₃ + 1.5·IQR.** (4) Any value BELOW the lower fence or ABOVE the upper fence is an **outlier**; compare the smallest and largest values to the fences.
Worked: IQR = 35 − 25 = **10**; 1.5 × 10 = 15; **LF = 25 − 15 = 10; UF = 35 + 15 = 50**; 18 > 10 and 40 < 50 → **no outliers**.
Example WITH an outlier: 32, 76, 80, 84, 86, 90, 92, 94 → Q₁ = 78, Q₃ = 91, IQR = 13, 1.5 × 13 = 19.5, LF = 58.5, UF = 110.5 → **32 < 58.5 → 32 is an outlier**.

### If asked: "draw / read a boxplot"
(1) Five-number summary + fences. (2) Number line; box from Q₁ to Q₃ with a line at the median. (3) Whiskers from the box out to the smallest and largest values that are NOT outliers (not to the fences). (4) Each outlier = a separate **\***. Commute data (to scale, 2 chars = 1 min; no outliers, so whiskers reach 18 and 40):

```
18            25        30.5      35        40
|-------------[==========|========]---------|
Min           Q1         M        Q3       Max
```

With an outlier (32, 76 … 94): box 78–91, line at 85, left whisker stops at 76 (the smallest NON-outlier), and 32 is drawn as \*. **Reading shape:** median in the middle of the box + equal whiskers → symmetric; median toward the left + long right whisker or right-side \* → skewed right (mirror → skewed left). Comparing two boxplots: compare medians (center), box widths/whiskers (spread), and outliers.

## THE 10 WAYS PEOPLE LOSE POINTS ON THIS EXAM
1. Not sorting before the median/quartiles. 2. Dividing by n instead of n − 1 for a SAMPLE variance (Q9: ÷4, not ÷5). 3. Stopping at the variance when SD was asked — take the √ and write the units. 4. Calculator: Sx vs σx mixed up; squaring a rounded SD. 5. Odd n: putting the median into the halves. 6. Class width 49 − 40 = 9 (it's 50 − 40 = 10). 7. Relative frequency as 30% instead of 0.3000; degrees without °. 8. Saying an observational study proves causation. 9. Naming skew by the hump instead of the TAIL. 10. Leaving "no mode" / "not applicable" blank — they ARE the answers. And: every number answer needs the steps written out.
